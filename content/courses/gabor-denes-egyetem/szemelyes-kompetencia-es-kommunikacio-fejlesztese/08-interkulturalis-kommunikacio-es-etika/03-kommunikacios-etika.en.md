## What you will learn

- the principles of **ethical communication**,
- why **data protection** and **confidentiality** matter in communication,
- what a **professional code of ethics** says,
- the role of **inclusive language**,
- how to use artificial intelligence **responsibly**.

## Principles

- **Honesty**: don't state falsehoods, and don't withhold essential information (e.g. hiding a known bug from the client).
- **Transparency**: make clear who is speaking, with what interest, and what the source is.
- **Respect**: preserve the other's dignity, even in a dispute.
- **Responsibility**: own the consequences of your words – and your mistakes.

> "I don't know, but I'll find out" or "I broke this, here's how I'll fix it" – in the long run these sentences increase your credibility.

## Data protection and confidentiality

As a computer engineer you often have access to **personal data** and **trade secrets**.

- In the EU, the **GDPR** regulates the handling of personal data: only the data needed, only for the stated purpose, securely.
- Don't send personal data (e.g. a customer list) unencrypted, in open email or in a public channel.
- Real user data, passwords and API keys must not end up in bug reports, screenshots or public forums (e.g. Stack Overflow).
- You often sign a **non-disclosure agreement** (NDA) with your employer: internal information can't go to social media either.

## Professional codes of ethics

The code of ethics of the **ACM**, the international association of computing professionals (_ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct_), asks among other things that you:

1. contribute to society and to human **well-being**,
2. **avoid harm**,
3. be **honest** and trustworthy,
4. be **fair** and do not discriminate,
5. respect the work required to produce new ideas and inventions (copyright),
6. respect **privacy**,
7. honour **confidentiality**.

## Inclusive language

Words can exclude or include.

- Avoid condescending, stereotypical remarks ("even my grandma gets this", "girls don't like coding").
- Don't assume others' gender or background: "the developer" doesn't always have to be "he".
- In professional communities neutral terms are increasingly used (e.g. _primary/replica_ instead of _master/slave_, _blocklist_ instead of _blacklist_).
- The point: respect the other's **dignity**, and listen if a term hurts someone.

## Responsible use of artificial intelligence

AI tools (chatbots, coding assistants) are useful but raise ethical questions:

- **Transparency**: if you made a text or code largely with AI and this matters for its use (e.g. university work, publication), disclose it according to the rules of that place.
- **Verification**: AI can be wrong with confidence (invented sources, faulty code) – the responsibility is yours.
- **Data protection**: don't paste confidential company code or personal data into an AI tool your employer hasn't approved.
- **Copyright and integrity**: presenting someone else's work as yours is plagiarism even if an AI "rephrased" it.

## Summary

- Ethical communication: honesty, transparency, respect, responsibility.
- Personal data and trade secrets only securely, as needed and for the purpose (GDPR, NDA).
- The ACM code: well-being, avoid harm, honesty, fairness, privacy, confidentiality.
- Inclusive language: respecting the other's dignity.
- AI: transparency, verification, data protection, integrity.
