## What you will learn

- the **principles** of a clear slide,
- the **10/20/30** rule,
- how to present **data** with a chart,
- typical **mistakes**.

## The slide is your helper, not your script

The audience either reads or listens to you – they can't do both at once. If everything you want to say is on the slide, they don't need you.

## Principles

- **One slide = one idea.** The slide title should be a statement ("Load time was cut in half"), not just a topic ("Results").
- **Little text**: keywords, not full sentences. As a guideline: at most about 6 lines, a few words each.
- **Readable font size**: visible from the back row (at least about 24 pt when projected).
- **Contrast**: dark text on a light background or vice versa; avoid red–green pairs (colour blindness).
- **Consistent** style: one or two fonts, few colours, the same layout.
- **White space**: an airy slide is easier to take in.

## The 10/20/30 rule

Guy Kawasaki's rule of thumb for investor pitches:

| Number | Meaning                     |
| ------ | --------------------------- |
| **10** | at most 10 slides           |
| **20** | at most 20 minutes          |
| **30** | at least 30-point font size |

It's not a law, but a good reminder: fewer but stronger slides.

## Data in charts

| What do you want to show?     | Good chart                           |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| change over time              | line chart                           |
| comparing categories          | column or bar chart                  |
| part of a whole (few parts)   | stacked bar (or pie, max 3–4 slices) |
| relationship of two variables | scatter plot                         |

Tips:

- **Highlight** the point (one colour for the interesting series, the rest grey).
- Put the **conclusion** in the title.
- Avoid **3D** and decorative effects – they distort.
- The y-axis of a bar chart should start at **zero**, otherwise you exaggerate the difference.

## Code and technical content on slides

- Show only the few **relevant** lines, large, with syntax highlighting.
- Highlight the line you are talking about.
- Draw architecture with simple **boxes and arrows**, not the full system design.

## Typical mistakes

- a slide full of text that the speaker reads out,
- an unreadable table with 12 columns,
- too many animations and transitions,
- a different style on every slide,
- a "Questions?" slide followed by minutes of silence (better leave the summary slide up).

## Summary

- One slide = one idea, with a statement as the title.
- Little text, large font, good contrast, consistent style.
- 10/20/30: fewer but stronger slides.
- Choose the chart type for the message and highlight the point.
