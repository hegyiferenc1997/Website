## What you will learn

- how to use your **voice** and **body** while presenting,
- how to handle **stage fright**,
- how to **rehearse**,
- how to answer **questions**,
- how to prepare a **live demo**.

## Voice

- **Pace**: slower than you think (stage fright speeds you up). On average about 120–150 words per minute is easy to follow.
- **Pauses**: pause 1–2 seconds before and after an important idea – it emphasises.
- **Volume**: speak to the people in the back row.
- **Variety**: in a monotone voice even the best content is boring.

## Body

- **Stable stance** on both feet; don't wander aimlessly, but you can step forward for a sentence.
- **Open gestures**, keep your hands visible; not in your pockets, not crossed.
- **Eye contact**: look around the room, at one person for 2–3 seconds at a time; don't talk to the projection screen.
- Smile when appropriate – the audience mirrors your energy.

## Stage fright

Stage fright is natural: the body switches to alert mode. You don't have to make it disappear, just manage it.

- **Prepare thoroughly** – uncertainty is its biggest source.
- Know the first 1–2 minutes almost word for word – after that you get going.
- **Breathing**: slow, deep exhaling (e.g. 4 s in, 6 s out) calms your heartbeat.
- Arrive early, test the equipment, chat with a few people from the audience.
- Remember: the audience is rooting **for** you, not against you.

## Rehearsal

- Say it **out loud**, at least 2–3 times, with a stopwatch.
- Record yourself on video and watch it (awkward, but very effective).
- Rehearse in front of a friend and ask for feedback.
- Don't memorise it all word for word – know the key ideas and transitions.

> How many minutes to plan for? If you have 10 minutes and say ~130 words per minute, that's about 1300 words – roughly two typed pages.

## Handling questions

1. **Listen to** the whole question, don't interrupt.
2. **Repeat** or briefly summarise it (so everyone hears it, and you gain a few seconds).
3. Answer **briefly**, to the whole audience.
4. If you don't know: "Good question, I don't know that exactly right now – I'll look it up and send it to you." This is far better than making something up.
5. With a provocative question, stay calm and factual; if needed, offer to continue after the talk.

## Live demo

A live demo is the strongest – and riskiest – part of a software talk.

- Have a **script**: know exactly what you will click.
- Use **prepared data**, don't type everything live.
- Increase the font size in the terminal and editor.
- Turn off notifications.
- Have a **plan B**: a screen recording or screenshots if the network or server lets you down.

## Summary

- Slow pace, pauses, varied voice; stable stance, open gestures, eye contact.
- Stage fright can be managed: preparation, a memorised opening, breathing.
- Rehearse out loud, with a stopwatch and video.
- Questions: listen, repeat, answer briefly; if you don't know, say so.
- Live demo: script, prepared data, plan B.
