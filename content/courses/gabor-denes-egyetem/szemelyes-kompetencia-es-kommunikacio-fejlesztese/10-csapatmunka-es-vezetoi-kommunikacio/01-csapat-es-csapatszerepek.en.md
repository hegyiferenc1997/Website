## What you will learn

- the difference between a **group** and a **team**,
- what a **well-functioning** team looks like,
- **Belbin's nine team roles**,
- how to set up roles in a **project team**.

## Group or team?

| Group                                | Team                                          |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| people work next to each other       | people work with each other                   |
| individual goals                     | a **shared goal** everyone is responsible for |
| individual responsibility            | individual **and** shared responsibility      |
| information flows through the leader | open, direct communication between members    |

A year group is a group; the four people working on an assignment become a team when they share a goal and build on each other.

## Hallmarks of a well-functioning team

- **A clear shared goal** – everyone knows what "done" means.
- **Clarified roles and responsibilities** – who is responsible for what.
- **Trust and psychological safety** – it's okay to ask and make mistakes (more in topic 11).
- **Open communication** and regular feedback.
- **Complementary skills** – not everyone needs to know the same things.

## Belbin's team roles

British researcher Meredith Belbin observed that in successful teams people fill different **behavioural roles**. He described nine roles in three groups:

### Action-oriented roles

| Role                   | Strength                                               | Weakness                            |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| **shaper**             | drives momentum, applies pressure, overcomes obstacles | impatient, may offend others        |
| **implementer**        | turns plans into practical steps, reliable             | rigid, slow to accept new things    |
| **completer finisher** | precise, looks for errors, finishes on time            | over-anxious, reluctant to delegate |

### People-oriented roles

| Role                      | Strength                                            | Weakness                                |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **coordinator**           | clarifies goals, delegates, pulls the team together | can seem manipulative, offloads work    |
| **teamworker**            | supportive, diplomatic, eases tension               | can be indecisive in critical moments   |
| **resource investigator** | builds contacts, explores opportunities             | loses interest after initial enthusiasm |

### Thought-oriented roles

| Role                  | Strength                                  | Weakness                           |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **plant**             | creative, new ideas, solves hard problems | ignores details                    |
| **monitor evaluator** | objective, critical, weighs every option  | uninspiring, can be slow           |
| **specialist**        | deep expertise in one area                | focuses only on their narrow field |

> Belbin roles are not personality types but **behavioural tendencies**. A person is usually strong in 2–3 roles and can fill others when needed.

## How to use them in a project team

- At the start of the project, discuss what each person is **strong** at and what they **want to learn**.
- See which role is **missing**: if nobody is a "completer finisher", everything will slip at the end; without a "plant", the solution will be dull.
- Discuss **weaknesses** openly too: "I know I tend to polish details for too long – tell me if needed."
- Record responsibilities (e.g. in a simple table: who owns the backend, the documentation, tracking deadlines).

## Summary

- A team has a shared goal and shared responsibility.
- A good team: clear goal, clear roles, trust, open communication, complementary skills.
- Belbin's nine roles: shaper, implementer, completer finisher; coordinator, teamworker, resource investigator; plant, monitor evaluator, specialist.
- A balanced team has all the important roles.
