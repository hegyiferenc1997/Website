## What you will learn

- how to give **constructive feedback** with the SBI model,
- how to **receive** feedback,
- how to **delegate** well,
- what **one-on-one** (1:1) meetings are for.

## Feedback with the SBI model

The **Center for Creative Leadership's** SBI model has three steps:

| Step              | Question                       | Example                                                                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **S – Situation** | When, where?                   | "At yesterday's sprint demo…"                                                   |
| **B – Behavior**  | What did they (observably) do? | "…when the client asked about performance, you answered with measurement data…" |
| **I – Impact**    | What effect did it have?       | "…and the client immediately approved the next phase."                          |

Negative feedback uses the same structure, and afterwards it's worth discussing the **next step** together:

> "At yesterday's standup (S) you interrupted the others three times when they talked about bugs (B). I noticed Dora didn't share her own problem afterwards (I). What do you think, how could we do this differently next time?"

Rules:

- Describe **concrete behaviour**, not personality ("you interrupted", not "you're disrespectful").
- **Timely**: as soon as possible, while you both remember.
- Criticism **one-on-one**.
- Not only negative: positive feedback should also be **specific** (what was good, rather than "good job").

> The "sandwich method" (praise – criticism – praise) often backfires: the other person only hears the praise, or learns that every praise is followed by a "but". Be straightforward and respectful instead.

## Receiving feedback

- **Listen to the end**, don't defend yourself immediately.
- **Ask** for clarification: "Could you give me a concrete example?"
- **Say thank you** – feedback is a gift, even when it's unpleasant.
- Think it over and decide what to do with it; come back to it later if needed.

## Delegation

To **delegate** means handing over a task **together with the authority and responsibility** that belong to it – not just "passing it on".

### What to delegate?

- what someone else can do **better** or **cheaper**,
- what **develops** the colleague,
- recurring tasks.

### Steps of good delegation

1. **Choose** the right person (ability, workload, development goal).
2. **Explain** the goal and the "why", not just the task.
3. Clarify the **expected result** and the **deadline** ("what counts as done?").
4. Give the necessary **authority** and resources.
5. Agree on **checkpoints** (e.g. a short catch-up on Monday).
6. **Don't take it back** if they get stuck – help, but let them solve it.
7. Give **feedback** and recognition at the end.

## One-on-one meetings (1:1)

A regular (e.g. weekly or fortnightly 30-minute) **1:1** between a manager and a team member is one of the most effective leadership tools.

- It is the **team member's time**: they bring the topics (blockers, development, ideas, problems).
- It's not a status report – that's what the ticket system and standup are for.
- Good questions: "What's blocking you most right now?", "What would you like to learn in the next six months?", "How can I help?"
- Note the agreements and come back to them next time.

## Summary

- SBI: situation, behaviour, impact – concrete, timely, criticism one-on-one.
- Receiving feedback: listen, clarify, say thank you.
- Delegation: goal, expected result, deadline, authority, checkpoints, recognition.
- 1:1: the team member's time, regular, focused on development and blockers.
