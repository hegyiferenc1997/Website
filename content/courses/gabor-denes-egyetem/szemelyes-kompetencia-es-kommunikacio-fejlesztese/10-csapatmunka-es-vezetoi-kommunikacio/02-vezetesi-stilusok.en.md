## What you will learn

- the difference between **leadership** and **management**,
- **Lewin's** three classic leadership styles,
- the **situational leadership** model,
- the basics of leadership communication.

## Leadership and management

- **Management**: planning, organising, allocating resources, controlling – "doing things right".
- **Leadership**: setting direction, motivating people, starting change – "doing the right things".

A team lead needs both. And you don't need a formal position to lead: in a university project anyone can **take the initiative**.

## Lewin's three styles

In the 1930s psychologist Kurt Lewin described these three styles:

| Style                          | Decision-making                                | Advantage                       | Disadvantage                   |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **autocratic** (directive)     | the leader decides alone                       | fast, effective in a crisis     | little commitment, creativity  |
| **democratic** (participative) | the leader involves the team                   | commitment, better ideas        | slower                         |
| **laissez-faire** (hands-off)  | the team decides, the leader barely intervenes | autonomy in an experienced team | chaos in an inexperienced team |

## Situational leadership (Hersey–Blanchard)

According to Paul Hersey and Ken Blanchard there is no single best style: the leader must adapt to the **follower's development level** (competence + commitment for the given task).

| Follower level                                    | Leadership style                    | What the leader does                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **D1** – beginner, enthusiastic, doesn't know how | **S1 – directing** (telling)        | precise instructions, close supervision    |
| **D2** – learning but disillusioned               | **S2 – coaching** (selling)         | explains, encourages, still decides        |
| **D3** – capable but unsure, unmotivated          | **S3 – supporting** (participating) | decide together, listens, encourages       |
| **D4** – experienced and confident                | **S4 – delegating**                 | hands over the task and the responsibility |

> Example: a new intern needs precise steps in the first week (S1). Six months later, for a familiar task, delegating is enough for the same intern (S4). Same person, different situation – different style.

## How a leader communicates

- **Direction and "why"**: the team should know not just what they do but why it matters (Simon Sinek: _Start with Why_).
- **Transparency**: share decisions and their reasons, bad news too.
- **Availability**: have time and a channel where people can ask questions.
- **Leading by example**: if the leader is late, doesn't document, doesn't ask for feedback, neither will the team.
- **Recognition**: praise specifically and publicly, criticise one-on-one.

## Summary

- Management: doing things right; leadership: doing the right things.
- Lewin: autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire.
- Situational leadership: directing → coaching → supporting → delegating, adapted to the follower's development level.
- Leadership communication: the "why", transparency, availability, leading by example, recognition.
