## What you will learn

- the difference between a **fixed** and a **growth mindset**,
- the five components of **emotional intelligence**,
- what **impostor syndrome** is and what you can do about it.

## Fixed and growth mindset

Based on psychologist Carol Dweck's research, we distinguish two basic attitudes:

| Fixed mindset                                                       | Growth mindset                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| "Either I'm talented at maths or I'm not."                          | "Maths can be improved with practice."         |
| avoids challenges, because failure "proves" they aren't good enough | seeks challenges, because they learn from them |
| hides mistakes                                                      | learns from mistakes                           |
| experiences criticism as a personal attack                          | treats criticism as information                |
| others' success feels threatening                                   | others' success feels inspiring                |

A growth mindset does **not** mean "anything is possible if you just want it", nor that you should only praise effort. It means abilities **can be developed** with the right strategy, practice and help.

> A simple trick: add the word "**yet**" to your sentences. "I don't understand recursion" → "I don't understand recursion yet."

## Emotional intelligence

Daniel Goleman popularised the concept of **emotional intelligence** (EQ): recognising, understanding and managing your own and others' emotions. Its five components:

| Component           | Meaning                                                     | Example at work                                                              |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **self-awareness**  | you recognise your own emotions and their effect            | "I'm stressed about the deadline, that's why I'm impatient with colleagues." |
| **self-regulation** | you manage your emotions, they don't carry you away         | you don't reply immediately and angrily to a sharp code review comment       |
| **motivation**      | inner drive, persistence through difficulties               | you keep working on a stubborn bug because you care about the solution       |
| **empathy**         | you understand others' feelings and perspectives            | you notice a teammate is overloaded and ask whether you can help             |
| **social skills**   | building good relationships, handling conflict, cooperating | you steer an argument towards a joint solution                               |

Emotional intelligence **can be developed**: through self-reflection, feedback, and by **pausing** before reacting to a strong emotion (e.g. not sending an angry email immediately but rereading it the next day).

## Impostor syndrome

**Impostor syndrome** (first described by Pauline Clance and Suzanne Imes) is the feeling that your successes are due only to luck or others' mistakes, and that you'll soon be "found out" as not really good enough. It is very common in computing – especially among beginners comparing themselves to experienced developers.

Signs:

- you deflect praise ("I was just lucky"),
- you over-prepare, or procrastinate out of fear of failure,
- you don't dare ask questions because "everyone else already knows".

What can you do?

- **Know you're not alone** – most experienced developers have felt it too.
- **Collect evidence**: a "success list" of solved problems and positive feedback.
- **Compare yourself with your past self**, not with others' best moments.
- **Talk about it** with a mentor or classmate.
- **Ask questions** – asking is part of learning, not a sign of weakness.

## Summary

- Growth mindset: abilities can be developed with practice and good strategies – "not yet".
- Emotional intelligence: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills.
- Impostor syndrome: common and manageable – collecting evidence, realistic comparisons, talking, asking.
