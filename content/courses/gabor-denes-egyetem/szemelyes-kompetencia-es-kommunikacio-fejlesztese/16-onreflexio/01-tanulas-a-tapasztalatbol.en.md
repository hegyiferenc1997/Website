## What is self-reflection?

**Self-reflection** is the **conscious examination** of your own thoughts, feelings, behaviour and experiences, with the aim of understanding and improving them.

A thought attributed to philosopher John Dewey: "We do not learn from experience; we learn from reflecting on experience." Two people can live through the same failure – the one who **thinks through** what happened and why grows more.

> Self-reflection isn't self-flagellation. The goal isn't to feel bad but to do better next time.

## Kolb's experiential learning cycle

According to David Kolb, learning is a four-step cycle:

```text
       1. concrete experience
          (I do something)
              ▲         │
              │         ▼
 4. active experimentation   2. reflective observation
 (I try the new approach)    (what happened? how did I experience it?)
              ▲         │
              │         ▼
       3. abstract conceptualisation
       (what did I learn? what's the general rule?)
```

Example: you write your first assignment (1), the feedback says the code is hard to read (2), you realise that descriptive variable names and smaller functions would help (3), you write the next assignment that way (4) – and the cycle starts again.

## Gibbs' reflective cycle

Graham Gibbs' six steps are useful for working through a specific event.

| Step               | Question                            | Example: a presentation that went badly                                         |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. description** | What happened?                      | I ran out of time at the project presentation and the demo didn't start.        |
| **2. feelings**    | What did I feel and think?          | Panic, embarrassment; then disappointment.                                      |
| **3. evaluation**  | What was good and what was bad?     | Good: the first part was clear. Bad: too many slides, no rehearsal.             |
| **4. analysis**    | Why did it turn out this way?       | I finished the night before, didn't time it, had no plan B for the demo.        |
| **5. conclusion**  | What could I have done differently? | Finish earlier, rehearse out loud, record the demo.                             |
| **6. action plan** | What will I do next time?           | Slides ready two days earlier, two timed rehearsals, a recorded demo as backup. |

## The learning journal

A **learning journal** (reflective journal) is a regular, short written review. 10 minutes a week is enough.

Some good questions:

- What did I learn this week?
- What went well, and why?
- What was hard, and what would I do differently?
- What feedback did I get, and what will I do with it?
- What is my next step?

As a developer your journal can be a simple text file or a private repo – many experienced developers keep such "TIL" (_Today I Learned_) notes.

## Summary

- Self-reflection is consciously thinking through experiences in order to grow – not self-flagellation.
- Kolb: experience → observation → conceptualisation → experimentation.
- Gibbs: description, feelings, evaluation, analysis, conclusion, action plan.
- A learning journal is a regular, short review.
