## What you will learn

- the four panes of the **Johari window**,
- how to do a **personal SWOT analysis**,
- how to explore your **values** and **strengths**.

## The Johari window

Joseph Luft and Harrington Ingham's model (named by combining their first names: Jo-Hari) shows how much we know about ourselves and how much others know about us.

```text
                    KNOWN TO SELF          NOT KNOWN TO SELF
              ┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
 KNOWN TO     │ OPEN AREA            │ BLIND SPOT           │
 OTHERS       │ what both I and      │ what others see,     │
              │ others know about me │ but I don't          │
              ├──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┤
 NOT KNOWN    │ HIDDEN AREA          │ UNKNOWN AREA         │
 TO OTHERS    │ what only I know     │ what nobody knows    │
              │ about myself         │ (yet)                │
              └──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
```

- **Open area**: e.g. "I'm good at explaining" – you and the team both know it.
- **Blind spot**: e.g. "you often interrupt others in meetings" – you don't notice, the others do.
- **Hidden area**: e.g. "I'm unsure about Git but don't dare admit it".
- **Unknown area**: hidden abilities that may emerge in a new situation.

The goal is to **enlarge the open area**:

- by **asking for feedback** you shrink the blind spot,
- by **self-disclosure** (in appropriate measure) you shrink the hidden area,
- by **new experiences** you discover the unknown.

## Personal SWOT analysis

The SWOT analysis companies use can be applied to yourself too:

|                                      | Helpful               | Harmful            |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Internal** (up to you)             | **S – strengths**     | **W – weaknesses** |
| **External** (from your environment) | **O – opportunities** | **T – threats**    |

A first-year student's example:

```text
Strengths:      I'm persistent; I already programmed in Python at school; I write well
Weaknesses:     I procrastinate; I don't like speaking in front of a group; my maths basics are weak
Opportunities:  a university special college; summer internship programmes; free online courses
Threats:        many parallel deadlines; a student job takes away study time
```

Then the point is the **conclusion**: how do you use your strengths to seize opportunities, and how do you reduce the impact of weaknesses and threats? E.g. "I write well → a technical blog for my portfolio"; "I don't like presenting → I'll volunteer for a short presentation in my group to practise".

## Values and strengths

- **Values**: what really matters to you? (e.g. learning, freedom, security, helping, creativity, fairness). If your work clashes with your values, you'll be dissatisfied in the long run – even if it pays well.
- **Strengths**: what are you good at, and what gives you energy? Notice which tasks make you lose track of time.

Questions for yourself:

- When was I proudest of myself in the past year, and why?
- What tasks do others ask me to help with?
- What would I do even if nobody paid me for it?

## Summary

- Johari window: open, blind spot, hidden, unknown – the goal is to enlarge the open area through feedback and self-disclosure.
- Personal SWOT: internal strengths and weaknesses, external opportunities and threats – and the conclusions you draw.
- Knowing your values and strengths helps you make good career decisions.
