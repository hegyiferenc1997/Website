## What you will learn

- the strengths and weaknesses of common **online channels**,
- **media richness theory**,
- how to choose a channel for a given **message**.

## Common channels

| Channel                                          | Strength                                            | Weakness                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **video call**                                   | faces, tone, fast feedback                          | needs scheduling, tiring, leaves no automatic record |
| **phone / voice call**                           | tone, fast                                          | no visual cues, no record                            |
| **chat** (Slack, Teams, Discord)                 | fast, informal, group                               | interrupts, gets lost among old messages             |
| **email**                                        | formal, also for external partners, leaves a record | slow, long threads                                   |
| **issue tracker / ticket** (Jira, GitHub Issues) | trackable task, owner, status                       | not meant for conversation                           |
| **wiki / documentation**                         | lasting knowledge, searchable                       | goes stale if not updated                            |

## Media richness theory

According to Richard Daft and Robert Lengel's theory, channels differ in "richness": how many cues (voice, face, gesture) they carry and how fast feedback is.

```text
rich ◄──────────────────────────────────────────────► lean
face-to-face  video call  phone  chat  email  document
```

- **Complex, ambiguous, emotional** messages (conflict, bad news, a complicated design question) need a **rich** channel.
- For **simple, unambiguous** messages (a time, a link, a short status) a **leaner**, faster channel is enough – even more efficient.

> A typical mistake: trying to resolve a conflict by email or chat. The exchange gets sharper and sharper, when a 10-minute call would have solved it.

## Decision aid

```text
Is the topic emotional or contentious?   → yes → face-to-face / video call
Does it need to leave a record?          → yes → email, ticket, wiki
A task with an owner and a deadline?     → ticket
A quick, short question for the team?    → chat (public channel, thread)
Knowledge that will be needed later?     → wiki / README
```

You often need to **combine** them: discuss the decision in a video call, then record it in an email or the wiki.

## Summary

- Every channel has its strengths: video and phone are fast and rich, written channels leave a record.
- Media richness: rich channels for complex, emotional messages, leaner ones are enough for simple ones.
- Record a decision in writing even if it was made verbally.

## Exercise

```quiz required
? A tense argument has developed with a colleague in chat. What is the best step?
- write an even longer chat message
+ suggest a short video call or face-to-face conversation
- write an email, with the whole team in Cc
- stop replying
! Emotional, contentious topics need a rich channel.

? According to media richness theory, which is the **richest** channel?
+ face-to-face conversation
- email
- chat
- written document
! The most cues and the fastest feedback.

? A bug needs to be reported that will have an owner and a deadline. Where should it go?
- a private chat message
+ the issue tracker (ticket)
- a phone call
- a social media post
! A trackable task, owner, status.

? You made a decision about hosting in a video call. What should you do afterwards?
- nothing, everyone remembers
+ record the decision in writing (email, wiki, notes)
- organise a new call about the same thing
- keep the decision secret
! Record decisions made verbally too.
```
