## What you will learn

- how to prepare for a **video call**,
- the rules for **camera, microphone and background**,
- what **video call fatigue** is,
- good practice in **chat**.

## Video call – preparation

- **Tech**: test the camera, microphone and app in advance (especially before a job interview).
- **Light**: the light source should be in front of you, not behind (otherwise only a silhouette shows).
- **Camera at eye level** – don't look up into it from below.
- **Background**: tidy or blurred; a virtual background is fine if it isn't distracting.
- **Headphones** – no echo, and people around you don't hear the conversation.
- **Notifications off**, especially before screen sharing.
- Join **1–2 minutes early**.

## Video call – during

- **Mute your microphone** when you're not speaking (especially in large groups).
- **Camera on** if that's the team's habit – in smaller meetings it helps a lot with feedback.
- Look at the **camera** when you speak, not at your own image.
- Signal when you want to speak: the **"raise hand"** feature, or in chat.
- Don't do **other things** meanwhile (email, phone) – it shows.
- If your connection drops, write in chat and rejoin.

## Video call fatigue

Many back-to-back video calls are more tiring than face-to-face meetings: we constantly look at faces (and our own image), nonverbal cues are harder to read, and we move less.

What can you do?

- Leave **breaks** between calls (e.g. 25- or 50-minute meetings instead of 30/60).
- Turn off **self-view**.
- If not needed, use a **voice call** or an asynchronous message.

## Chat – good practice

| Rule                                              | Why?                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| reply in a **thread**                             | the channel stays readable                                             |
| write the question **in one message**             | not five short messages that ping five times                           |
| `@mention` only if you expect something from them | `@everyone` / `@channel` disturbs everyone                             |
| use a **status** (busy, on leave, focus)          | others know when they can count on you                                 |
| respect **working hours**                         | schedule late-evening messages for the morning, or say it's not urgent |
| **emoji reactions** to acknowledge (👍, ✅)       | no need for a separate "ok" message                                    |
| send code in a **code block**                     | readable, doesn't get mangled                                          |

> Emojis make up for missing nonverbal cues, but their meaning depends on culture and age group. In formal situations (clients, strangers) use them sparingly.

## Summary

- Before a video call: tech, light, camera at eye level, background, notifications off.
- During: mute, look at the camera, raise hand, pay attention.
- Against video call fatigue: breaks, self-view off, fewer calls.
- Chat: threads, the question in one message, targeted mentions, status, respect for working hours.
