## What you will learn

- **Tuckman's** model of group development,
- the **signs** of each stage,
- what you can do to help the team **move on**.

## Tuckman's model

In 1965 psychologist Bruce Tuckman described four, later five stages that a new team typically goes through:

```text
forming → storming → norming → performing → adjourning
```

| Stage          | Characteristics                                                | Task of the team / leader                                       |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **forming**    | politeness, caution, uncertainty about goals and roles         | clarify goals and framework; get to know each other             |
| **storming**   | disputes about methods, roles, leadership; tension             | handle conflicts openly; listen; agree on shared rules          |
| **norming**    | shared rules and working methods emerge, trust grows           | record agreements; hand over responsibility                     |
| **performing** | effective, autonomous work, the team solves problems itself    | delegate; development opportunities; recognition                |
| **adjourning** | end of the project, farewell, sometimes sadness or uncertainty | celebrate results; lessons learned (retrospective); say goodbye |

## Storming is not a failure

Many teams get scared when disputes appear – yet **storming is natural**, and without it no real shared norms emerge. If a team avoids disputes, it often gets stuck in "false harmony", and problems explode later, at a worse moment.

What helps?

- disputes should be about the **task**, not about people (see topic 7),
- have a **shared decision rule** (e.g. "if we can't agree in 15 minutes, the tech lead decides"),
- **write down** agreements (e.g. coding rules, the definition of "done", response time in chat).

## Regression

The stages aren't always one-way. The arrival of a **new member**, a **change of leader** or a **big change** (e.g. a new technology) can take the team back to forming or storming.

## A university project example

- **Week 1 – forming**: everyone is nice, nobody wants to argue, it's unclear who does what.
- **Weeks 2–3 – storming**: a dispute about the framework, someone doesn't do their part, tension.
- **Week 4 – norming**: you agree on a weekly meeting, a shared task board and a code review rule.
- **Weeks 5–10 – performing**: work progresses, problems are discussed quickly.
- **After submission – adjourning**: a presentation, a dinner together, and a short talk about what you'd do differently.

## Summary

- Tuckman: forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning.
- Storming is natural; disputes about the task lead to shared norms.
- Change (new member, new leader) can take the team back to an earlier stage.
- At adjourning, celebrate and draw lessons.
