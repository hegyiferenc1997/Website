## What you will learn

- what **psychological safety** is and why it is the foundation of a good team,
- the purpose of **agile** team meetings,
- how to run an **effective meeting**,
- the rules of **asynchronous** teamwork.

## Psychological safety

According to Amy Edmondson's definition, **psychological safety** is the shared belief that the team is **safe for interpersonal risk-taking**: you can ask questions, admit mistakes, share ideas, disagree – without being embarrassed or punished.

According to Google's _Project Aristotle_ research (2012–2015), this was the **most important** factor distinguishing successful teams from the rest.

How can it be built?

- The leader **admits their own mistakes** and uncertainty.
- Questions are **thanked for**, not laughed at ("Good that you asked, surely others didn't get it either.").
- The team **learns** from mistakes instead of looking for a culprit (blameless post-mortems are covered in topic 18).
- Everyone gets to speak in meetings.

## Agile team meetings (Scrum)

| Event                           | When?                     | Purpose                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **sprint planning**             | start of the sprint       | what we will deliver in the next 1–4 weeks, and how             |
| **daily standup** (daily scrum) | every day, max 15 minutes | alignment: progress towards the goal, blockers                  |
| **sprint review**               | end of the sprint         | demonstrating finished work to stakeholders, feedback           |
| **retrospective**               | end of the sprint         | how to work better: what went well, what didn't, what we change |

The daily standup is **not a status report to the manager** but the team's coordination among themselves. Detailed problem-solving happens afterwards, separately, with the people involved.

A simple retrospective format:

```text
What went well?   | What didn't go well?  | What will we try next time?
------------------|-----------------------|------------------------------
fast code review  | the client's need was | clarify tasks with examples
                  | discovered late       | during planning
```

## Effective meetings

- **Is it needed at all?** If an email or a chat message will do, don't call a meeting.
- **Agenda** sent in advance, with a goal: "At the end we decide on hosting."
- **Only the people involved** should attend.
- **Timebox** it, and stick to it.
- Someone **takes notes**: decisions, action items with owner and deadline (see topic 6).
- **Summary** at the end: what we decided, who does what.

> A one-hour meeting with 8 participants costs 8 working hours. Is it worth it?

## Asynchronous teamwork

In remote work and across time zones, much of a team's communication is **written**.

- **Write complete messages**: not just "Hi, got a minute?", but the question, the context and what you've already tried, right away.
- Reply in **threads** so the channel stays readable.
- Ask in **public channels**, not privately – others learn from it and more people can help.
- Agree on **response times** (e.g. in chat by the end of the working day, by email within 1 working day) and on what counts as **urgent** (and which channel to use for it).
- **Document** decisions and knowledge (wiki, README), don't leave them only in chat.

## Summary

- Psychological safety is the most important characteristic of successful teams.
- Scrum events: planning, daily standup, review, retrospective.
- Effective meeting: goal, agenda, the right people, timebox, notes, summary.
- Asynchronous work: complete messages, threads, public channels, agreed response times, documentation.
