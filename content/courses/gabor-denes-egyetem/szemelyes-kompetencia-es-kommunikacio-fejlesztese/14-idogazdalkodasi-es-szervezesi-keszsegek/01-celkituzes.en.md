## Why do we need goals?

Time management isn't about doing as many things as possible, but about having time for **the important things**. For that you need to know what is important – that's what goals tell you.

According to Edwin Locke and Gary Latham's research, **specific and challenging** goals lead to better performance than vague "do your best" goals.

## SMART goals

| Letter | Meaning    | Question                                 |
| ------ | ---------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **S**  | specific   | What exactly do I want to achieve?       |
| **M**  | measurable | How will I know I succeeded?             |
| **A**  | achievable | Is it realistic in my current situation? |
| **R**  | relevant   | Why does it matter to me?                |
| **T**  | time-bound | By when?                                 |

**Bad goal:** "I want to get better at programming."

**SMART goal:** "By 15 December I'll finish my Python-based expense tracker and upload it to GitHub, with at least 10 automated tests."

## OKR – objectives and key results

The **OKR** (_Objectives and Key Results_) method was developed at Intel and made famous by Google. An **inspiring objective** comes with 2–5 **measurable key results**.

```text
Objective: Be able to build web applications confidently by the end of the semester.

Key results:
  KR1: Complete an online course on the Flask framework (12 modules).
  KR2: Build 2 working web apps and publish them.
  KR3: Ask at least 3 more experienced developers for a code review.
```

The objective is **qualitative** and motivating, the key results are **quantitative** and checkable.

## Breaking down a big goal

A semester-long goal is too big to just start on tomorrow morning. Break it down:

```text
semester goal  →  monthly milestones  →  weekly tasks  →  daily next step
```

The most important question every day: **"What is the next concrete step?"** – "write the login form" instead of "work on the project".

## Summary

- Specific, challenging goals lead to better performance.
- SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound.
- OKR: an inspiring objective + 2–5 measurable key results.
- Break big goals down into concrete daily next steps.
