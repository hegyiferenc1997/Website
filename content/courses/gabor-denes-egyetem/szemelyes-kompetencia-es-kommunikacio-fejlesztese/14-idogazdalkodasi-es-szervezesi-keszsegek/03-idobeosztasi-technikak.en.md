## The Pomodoro technique

Francesco Cirillo's method (named after a tomato-shaped kitchen timer):

1. Choose **one** task.
2. Set **25 minutes** and work only on it – notifications off.
3. Take a **5-minute** break.
4. After **four** pomodoros, take a longer **15–30-minute** break.

Why does it work? A short, bounded time makes starting easier, breaks keep you fresh, and the number of pomodoros shows how much time a task takes.

> For programming, 25 minutes is sometimes too short to "get into it" – feel free to use a 50 + 10 minute rhythm.

## Time blocking

You reserve **time blocks** in your calendar for important tasks, as if they were meetings:

```text
Tuesday
08:00–10:00  ███ Programming assignment (deep work, phone in another room)
10:00–12:00  ░░░ Lecture
13:00–13:30  ▒▒▒ Emails, messages
13:30–15:00  ███ Maths practice
17:00–18:00  ▓▓▓ Workout
```

- **Deep work** (Cal Newport: _Deep Work_) needs longer, uninterrupted blocks.
- Batch **shallow work** (email, chat, admin) into one or two blocks, not scattered through the day.
- Every context switch costs time until you get back into the flow.

## GTD – Getting Things Done

The essence of David Allen's method: **don't store to-dos in your head**, because it causes constant stress.

1. **Capture**: write every to-do and idea in a trusted place (app, notebook).
2. **Clarify**: what is it, and what's the next step? If it can be done **within 2 minutes**, do it right away.
3. **Organise**: lists (next actions, waiting for someone, someday/maybe), calendar.
4. **Review** weekly.
5. **Engage**.

## Parkinson's law

C. Northcote Parkinson's observation: **"Work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion."** If you have two weeks for an assignment, it takes two weeks; if two days, two days.

That's why it's worth setting **your own, earlier deadlines** and timeboxes ("I'll write this part in 2 hours today").

## Procrastination

Procrastination isn't laziness; most often it is an **emotion-regulation** problem: we avoid the unpleasant feeling (boredom, anxiety, uncertainty, fear of failure) the task triggers.

| Cause                          | What you can do                                            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| the task is too big, unclear   | break it down; write the next 5-minute step                |
| you don't know how to start    | ask; look at an example; start with the easiest part       |
| perfectionism, fear of failure | allow yourself a "bad first draft"                         |
| it's boring                    | Pomodoro; a reward afterwards; do it together with someone |
| distractions                   | phone in another room, notifications off, website blocker  |

The **5-minute rule**: tell yourself you'll only work on it for 5 minutes. Starting is the hardest part – after that you usually keep going.

## Summary

- Pomodoro: 25 minutes of work, 5 minutes break, a longer break after four.
- Time blocking: long blocks for deep work, batch shallow work.
- GTD: capture, clarify (2-minute rule), organise, review, engage.
- Parkinson: work fills the time available – set your own deadlines.
- Procrastination is an emotional problem: break it down, start small, remove distractions.
