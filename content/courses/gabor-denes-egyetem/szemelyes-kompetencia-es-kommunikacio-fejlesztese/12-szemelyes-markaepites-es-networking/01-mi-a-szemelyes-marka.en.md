## What you will learn

- what a **personal brand** means,
- how to phrase your own **value proposition**,
- why **authenticity** and **consistency** matter,
- what you can do **as a first-year**.

## The personal brand

A saying attributed to Jeff Bezos: "Your brand is what other people say about you when you're not in the room."

A **personal brand** is the impression people in your professional environment form of you: what you're good at, what they can expect from you, what values you represent. **Everyone has one** – the only question is whether you shape it consciously.

> A personal brand isn't bragging or a "fake image". It works when it is **authentic**: it shows who you are and what you really can do.

## Why bother?

- Recruiters and future teammates **search** for your name (LinkedIn, GitHub, Google).
- A strong professional profile attracts **opportunities**: internships, project invitations, speaking requests.
- It helps **you** clarify where you want to grow.

## The value proposition

A **value proposition** is one or two sentences about **for whom** and **in what** you can add value, and **what makes you special**.

A simple template:

```text
I help [whom] [achieve what] by [how / what I'm strong at].
```

Examples for students:

- "I'm a computer engineering student going deep into backend development; I like building fast, well-tested APIs in Python."
- "As a first-year I'm interested in accessible web interfaces everyone can use; I'm currently designing the interface of a university timetable app."

## The three pillars of a brand

| Pillar          | Question                 | How you build it                                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **expertise**   | What are you good at?    | projects, learning, competitions, open source    |
| **visibility**  | Do others know about it? | profiles, talks, blog, meetups                   |
| **credibility** | Can they trust you?      | kept promises, honest communication, consistency |

## Consistency

Your profiles (CV, LinkedIn, GitHub, personal website) should show **the same picture**: the same name, a similar profile photo, matching descriptions. If your CV says "Python developer", there should be Python projects on your GitHub.

## How to start?

1. Write down what **interests** you in the field and what you'd like to be good at in 2–3 years.
2. Draft the first version of your **value proposition** (it can change later!).
3. Create or update **one or two profiles** (LinkedIn, GitHub).
4. Choose **one project** you'll see through and can show.
5. **Share** what you learn – a short post is enough.

## Summary

- A personal brand is the professional impression others form of you – everyone has one.
- Value proposition: for whom, in what you add value, what makes you special.
- Pillars: expertise, visibility, credibility.
- Be consistent across your profiles, and authentic.
