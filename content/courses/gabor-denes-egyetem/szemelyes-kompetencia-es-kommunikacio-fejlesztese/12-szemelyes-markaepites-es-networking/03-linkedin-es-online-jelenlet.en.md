## What you will learn

- the elements of a good **LinkedIn profile**,
- how to build relationships **online**,
- how to **share** your knowledge,
- how to manage your **digital footprint**.

## The LinkedIn profile

| Element              | Tip                                                                                               |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **profile photo**    | well lit, friendly, professional photo with your face clearly visible                             |
| **background image** | a simple image related to your field                                                              |
| **headline**         | not just "student": "Computer engineering student · Python, backend · open to internships"        |
| **About**            | 3–5 sentences: your value proposition, interests, what you're working on, what you're looking for |
| **experience**       | student jobs, internships, volunteering, teaching assistant work – with results                   |
| **projects**         | with a link to GitHub or the demo                                                                 |
| **education**        | university, programme, relevant courses                                                           |
| **skills**           | the top 5–10 you can also talk about                                                              |
| **custom URL**       | linkedin.com/in/ben-kiss – can go in your CV                                                      |

## Online networking

- Connect with people you've **met** (meetup, internship, your year) – with a **personal message** (see topic 9).
- Follow the **companies** and **professionals** you're interested in.
- **Respond** meaningfully to others' posts: a thoughtful comment is worth more than a like.
- Join **professional communities**: Discord servers, open-source projects, forums.
- Ask for a **recommendation** from someone you worked with – and write some for others too.

## Sharing content

You don't need to be an influencer. A short post now and then about what you **learned** or **built** shows your expertise and growth.

Ideas:

- "This weekend I took part in a hackathon, here's what we built and what I learned."
- "Three things I understood while doing my first Docker project."
- Presenting a project with a screenshot or short video.
- Recommending a useful resource, with your own opinion.

Rules:

- **Add value**, don't just brag.
- Be **accurate** – a wrong technical claim damages your brand.
- **Confidentiality**: internal workplace information must not get out (see topic 8).
- Ask permission if others appear in the post.

## The digital footprint

Everything you do online (posts, photos, comments) leaves a trace and **can remain for years**.

- **Search for your name** in a private browser window – this is what a recruiter sees too.
- Review your **old social profiles**: what's public, what isn't? Adjust the privacy settings.
- Separate **professional** and **private** presence if you like (e.g. LinkedIn professional, Instagram private).
- Before posting, think: **would you stand by this in a job interview in 5 years**?
- Stay **respectful** in online arguments too – screenshots live forever.

## Summary

- LinkedIn: good photo, telling headline, About with your value proposition, results, projects, custom URL.
- Online networking: personal messages, meaningful responses, communities, recommendations.
- Regularly share what you learned or built – adding value, accurately, respecting confidentiality.
- Your digital footprint is lasting: check it, and post consciously.
