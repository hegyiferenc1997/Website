## What you will learn

- what **networking** really means,
- what the **strength of weak ties** is,
- what the **hidden job market** is,
- what **Dunbar's number** says,
- where you can start **right now**.

## What is networking?

**Networking** is the deliberate building and maintaining of professional relationships based on **mutual** benefit: information, learning, opportunities, help.

> It isn't collecting business cards or sending LinkedIn invitations to every stranger. A good relationship is built on **trust**, and on you giving too.

## The strength of weak ties

According to sociologist Mark Granovetter's 1973 research, people hear about new jobs more often from **acquaintances** (weak ties) than from close friends (strong ties).

Why? Your close friends know roughly the same people and information as you. Your acquaintances move in **other circles**, so they bring new information.

| Strong tie                | Weak tie                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| friends, family, roommate | a fellow student from another programme, a meetup acquaintance, a former fellow intern |
| many mutual acquaintances | few mutual acquaintances                                                               |
| emotional support         | new information, new opportunities                                                     |

## The hidden job market

Many positions are **not advertised** publicly, or by the time they are, there is already an internally referred candidate. Companies like hiring through **referrals**, because the referrer vouches with their own reputation.

So connections don't mean "favouritism" but that people **know about you** and what you can do.

## Dunbar's number

According to anthropologist Robin Dunbar, a person can maintain roughly **150** stable social relationships – people they know well enough to know who is who and how they relate to each other. Within that, the closest circle is about 5, then 15 and 50 people.

The lesson: you may have 500 LinkedIn connections, but you can only truly **maintain** a few. Quality matters more than quantity.

## Where can you start right now?

- **Fellow students** – in 5–10 years they will be colleagues, team leads, founders.
- **Lecturers, teaching assistants**, department projects.
- **Student organisations**, special colleges, the student union.
- **Hackathons**, programming competitions.
- **Meetups**, professional conferences (often with student tickets or volunteer options).
- The communities of **open-source** projects.
- **Internships**, student jobs.

## Summary

- Networking is mutual, trust-based professional relationship building.
- Weak ties bring new information and opportunities.
- Many jobs are filled through referrals (hidden job market).
- According to Dunbar's number (~150), the quality of relationships matters.
- You can start as a first-year: fellow students, lecturers, hackathons, meetups.
