## What you will learn

- how to **follow up** after making contact,
- why **reciprocity** is fundamental,
- how to **keep** relationships alive,
- how to find a **mentor**.

## Following up

Most connections are lost because **nobody gets in touch** after the event.

- Send a short message **within 48 hours** (LinkedIn or email).
- Refer to the conversation: "Glad we talked about Rust at the meetup."
- If you promised something (a link to an article, a repo), **send it**.

## Give and take

Networking isn't about "what can I get". According to Adam Grant's research (_Give and Take_), in the long run **givers** (who like to help but also keep boundaries) do best.

What can you give as a student?

- a useful **article** or tool the other person mentioned,
- **feedback** on a project or blog post,
- an **introduction**: "I know someone who works on exactly this."
- **congratulations** on a promotion or a successful release,
- help in an open-source project.

## Keeping relationships alive

- **Get in touch** occasionally even without a reason (but not intrusively): an interesting piece of news, a question.
- If someone helped you, **tell them** later what came of it: "Based on your advice I applied, and I got in!"
- Keep a simple **list** of important contacts (who, where you met, what you talked about) – a spreadsheet is enough.
- Don't only contact someone when you need them.

## Finding a mentor

A **mentor** is a more experienced professional who supports your development with advice and feedback.

- It could be a lecturer, a senior student, a senior developer at your internship, the maintainer of an open-source project.
- Many universities and companies have **mentoring programmes**.
- Don't start with "Would you be my mentor?" – that's a big ask. Start with a **specific question**, and if it works well, the relationship deepens by itself.

### A good mentoring relationship

| Mentee (you)                                     | Mentor                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| arrive with specific questions                   | asks questions, doesn't just give the answer |
| respect their time, prepare                      | shares experience, including mistakes        |
| report back what you implemented from the advice | gives honest feedback                        |
| are open to criticism                            | recommends contacts                          |

> **Reverse mentoring**: as a student you can teach too – for example, showing an experienced colleague a new tool or technology.

## Summary

- Follow up within 48 hours, referring to the conversation.
- Give too: articles, feedback, introductions, congratulations.
- Keep relationships alive, don't only reach out when you need something.
- Approach a mentor with a specific question; a good mentee is prepared and reports back.
