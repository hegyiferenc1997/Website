## What you will learn

- how to **prepare** for a professional event,
- how to write a **30-second introduction** (elevator pitch),
- how to start and continue **small talk**,
- how to **end** a conversation politely,
- how to make contact **online**.

## Preparing for an event

- Check the **speakers** and **participating companies** – who would you like to talk to, and why?
- Have **1–2 questions** about the talks or companies.
- Set yourself a small goal: "Today I'll talk to three new people."
- Update your **LinkedIn** profile (see topic 12) so people can find you after the conversation.

## The elevator pitch

A short, roughly **30-second** introduction that says who you are, what you do and what interests you.

```text
Who you are:    "Hi, I'm Ben Kiss, a first-year computer engineering student."
What you do:    "I'm working on a Python-based timetable planner with my classmates."
What interests you: "I'm really into backend development, that's why I came to this meetup."
Connection:     "What are you working on at the moment?"
```

- Keep it **natural**, not a memorised speech.
- Adapt it to the situation (you say something different to a recruiter than to a fellow student).
- End with a **question** so the other person can talk too.

## Small talk

Small talk isn't pointless chit-chat: it is the first step of trust.

**Good openers** build on the shared situation:

- "What did you think of the last talk?"
- "Is this your first time here? It's my first."
- "I saw you work at [company] – what's development like there?"

**How to keep it going?**

- Ask **open questions** (see topic 3).
- **Listen** to the answer and respond to it – don't think about your next question.
- Look for **common ground** (same technology, university, hobby).
- Share something about yourself too – conversation is two-way.

**Topics to avoid** in a first conversation: politics, religion, salary, gossiping about others.

## Ending a conversation gracefully

You don't have to talk to one person all evening. A polite exit:

- "Really glad we talked! I'll look you up on LinkedIn, if that's okay."
- "I'd like to catch one of the speakers, but I hope we'll run into each other again."
- Introduce them to a third person: "Do you know Anna? She works on machine learning too."

## Joining a group

Join a talking group when their circle is **open** (there's a gap, they aren't standing closed, facing each other). Stand there, listen a little, then join the topic at a suitable moment.

## Making contact online

- Add a **personal message** to a **LinkedIn** invitation: where you know each other from and why you're reaching out.

```text
"Dear Anna Toth, yesterday at the Budapest.py meetup I heard your talk on type
annotations – it was very useful. I'd be glad to connect and follow your work.
Best, Ben Kiss"
```

- Don't ask for a job or a favour in the first message.
- In a professional community (Discord, forum) **give** first: answer questions, share what you've learned.

## Summary

- Prepare: who will be there, what you'd ask, what your goal is.
- Elevator pitch: who you are, what you do, what interests you, a question – about 30 seconds.
- Small talk: open from the shared situation, open questions, attention, common ground.
- End politely, and follow up online with a personal message.
