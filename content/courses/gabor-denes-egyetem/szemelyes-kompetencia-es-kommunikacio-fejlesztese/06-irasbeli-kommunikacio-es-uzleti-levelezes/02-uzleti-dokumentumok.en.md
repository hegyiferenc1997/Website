## What you will learn

- how to write **notes** after a meeting,
- the structure of a **status report / report**,
- the basics of a good **CV** and **cover letter**.

## Meeting notes (memo)

The value of a meeting lies in its **decisions and action items** – if nobody writes them down, in a week everyone remembers something different.

```text
Notes – Project kick-off meeting
Date: 12 Nov 2026, 10:00–10:45
Participants: Anna Nagy, Ben Kiss, Cecilia Toth, David Varga

Decisions
1. The backend will be Node.js + PostgreSQL.
2. We work in two-week sprints, first sprint: 16 Nov.

Action items
| Task                          | Owner     | Deadline |
| ----------------------------- | --------- | -------- |
| set up repo and CI            | Ben       | 14 Nov   |
| organise user interviews      | Cecilia   | 20 Nov   |

Open questions
- Does the first version need an English UI? → Anna checks with the client.
```

- Send it **within 24 hours** of the meeting.
- An action item always has an **owner** and a **deadline**.

## Status reports and reports

Managers and clients spend little time on a report, so the **executive summary** comes first.

1. **Executive summary** – 3–5 sentences: where we are, the main result, the main risk, our request.
2. **Background** – why the report was made.
3. **Results / data** – with charts and tables.
4. **Risks and issues**.
5. **Recommendation / next steps**.
6. **Appendices** – detailed data.

For weekly status reports a **"traffic light"** works well: green (on plan), amber (risk), red (slipping, intervention needed).

## CV (résumé)

A recruiter often looks at a CV for only half a minute at first. Therefore:

- **1 page** as a graduate; clean, simple layout, readable font.
- At the top: name, contact details (email, phone), **LinkedIn** and **GitHub** links.
- **Reverse chronological order**: the most recent experience first.
- **Projects** – as a university student this is your strongest section: what you built, with what technology, what the result was.
- **Results, not duties**: "I introduced caching, response time dropped from 800 ms to 120 ms" instead of "I worked on backend development".
- **Skills**: only list what you can also discuss in an interview.
- Languages with level (e.g. English B2).
- Tailor it to the job ad: include the ad's keywords (if true for you) – many companies use automated screening (ATS).

## Cover letter

A short (about half to one page), personalised letter:

1. **Which position** you are applying for and **why this company** (show that you know them).
2. **What you bring**: 2–3 concrete examples from your projects related to the ad.
3. **Closing**: enthusiasm, and that you'd be glad to introduce yourself at an interview.

> Don't repeat your CV – the cover letter answers "why" and "how do I fit here".

## Summary

- Meeting notes: decisions, action items with owner and deadline, open questions; within 24 hours.
- Report: executive summary first, then background, results, risks, recommendation.
- CV: 1 page, reverse chronological, projects, measurable results, tailored to the ad.
- Cover letter: why here, what you bring, short and personalised.

- participants' opinions of each other
- the times of the coffee breaks
! These show what happens next.

? Where does the **executive summary** go in a report?
+ at the very beginning
- after the appendices
- in the middle
- nowhere, it's not needed
! The manager might only read this.

? Which is the better CV line?
- "I worked on backend development."
+ "I introduced caching, response time dropped from 800 ms to 120 ms."
- "I did lots of things."
- "I was responsible for various tasks."
! A concrete, measurable result.

? In what order should experience appear in a CV?
- chronological, starting with the oldest
+ reverse chronological, starting with the most recent
- alphabetical
- random
! The most recent is the most relevant.
