## What you will learn

- how to write a **good subject line**,
- the **structure** of a business email,
- the correct use of **To / Cc / Bcc**,
- the most common **mistakes**.

## When to write an email?

Email is good when the message needs to **leave a trace**, when it is for an **external** partner, or when the recipient needs time to reply. For quick, informal coordination the team chat is better; for sensitive or contentious topics a face-to-face conversation.

## The subject line

Based on the subject line the recipient decides when (and whether) to open it.

| Bad            | Good                                                     |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Question"     | "Question: submission format of the thesis topic"        |
| "Important!!!" | "Decision needed by Friday: choosing a hosting provider" |
| (empty)        | "Invitation: project kick-off meeting, 12 Nov, 10:00"    |

Make it **specific**, and if there's an action, refer to it.

## The structure of an email

```text
Subject: Request: access to the test database by Thursday

Dear Mr Smith,                                       ← greeting

I'm working on the new search in the mobile app,     ← context (1-2 sentences)
and I need realistically sized data for the performance test.

Could I get read access to the test database          ← the point / request
by Thursday noon?

If that's not possible, an anonymised extract          ← details, alternative
would also do (about 100,000 rows).

Thank you in advance!                                 ← closing

Best regards,
Anna Nagy                                            ← signature
Developer, Mobile team | +36 30 123 4567
```

- **The point first**: the recipient should understand from the first two lines what you want.
- **One email – one topic.** If you have three topics, write three emails (or number the questions).
- **Short paragraphs**, bullet points if needed.
- If there's an action, give a **deadline** and an **owner**.

## Greeting and tone

- In English: "Dear Mr/Ms Smith," – for an unknown recipient "Dear Hiring Manager,".
- In Hungarian formal letters: "Tisztelt Kovács Péter!" or "Kedves Kovács Péter!".
- Match the recipient's style: if they reply informally, you can be informal too.
- Avoid ALL CAPS (SHOUTING), lots of exclamation marks and irony – in writing it is easily misunderstood.

## To, Cc, Bcc

| Field   | For whom?                                                                               |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **To**  | the people you expect a reply or action from                                            |
| **Cc**  | people you want to inform, but expect no action from                                    |
| **Bcc** | people whose addresses others must not see (e.g. a mailing to many external recipients) |

> For a mailing to many external recipients use Bcc or a mailing list: exposing an open address list can be a data protection problem.

Only use **"Reply all"** if your reply really concerns everyone.

## Attachments and response time

- Refer to the attachment in the text ("I've attached the report as a PDF.") and check before sending that you really attached it.
- Instead of a large file, send a **shared link**.
- At work it is usually polite to reply within 1 working day – if you can't reply properly, say so: "Got it, I'll reply by Thursday."

## Before sending

1. Subject, greeting, signature in place?
2. The point in the first two lines?
3. Spelling, names, dates correct?
4. Right recipient list?
5. Attachment?

## Summary

- Specific subject line; the point first; one email – one topic.
- An adaptive, polite tone; avoid irony in writing.
- To: action; Cc: information; Bcc: hidden addresses.
- Check recipients and attachments before sending.
