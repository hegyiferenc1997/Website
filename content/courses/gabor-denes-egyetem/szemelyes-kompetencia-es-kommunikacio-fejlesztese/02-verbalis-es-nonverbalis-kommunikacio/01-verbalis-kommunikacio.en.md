## What you will learn

- what belongs to **verbal** communication,
- how to choose **words** for your audience,
- what **paraverbal** cues are and what they reveal.

## Verbal communication

**Verbal** communication is the use of language, that is, **words** – both spoken and written. It includes:

- **vocabulary** and word choice,
- **sentence structure** (short or long, simple or complex sentences),
- **register** (formal, everyday, technical, slang).

### Adapt to your audience

You explain the same content differently to a senior developer, a project manager and your grandmother.

| Listener        | Example                                                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| developer       | "Cache invalidation was broken, so the API returned stale JSON."          |
| project manager | "A bug made the site show old data; we fixed it, it lasted 10 minutes."   |
| grandmother     | "The website showed outdated information for a while, but it's fine now." |

### Clear speech

- **Concrete** words instead of general ones: "by 10 tomorrow" instead of "soon".
- **Active** voice: "Peter fixed the bug" instead of "the bug was fixed".
- Avoid **filler words** ("well", "like", "basically", "you know").
- Use abbreviations and jargon only if the listener surely knows them – or explain them the first time.

## Paraverbal communication

**Paraverbal** cues relate to how speech sounds – not _what_ you say, but _how_ it sounds.

| Cue              | What it may signal                                                 |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **volume**       | too quiet: uncertainty; too loud: aggression                       |
| **pace**         | too fast: nervousness, hurry; too slow: boredom                    |
| **pitch**        | monotone: lack of interest; varied: enthusiasm                     |
| **stress**       | which word matters ("_I_ didn't say it" vs. "I didn't _say_ it")   |
| **pause**        | emphasises, gives time to understand; too many pauses sound unsure |
| **articulation** | clarity, confidence                                                |

> Try it: say "I think this is a good idea" enthusiastically, sarcastically and in a bored voice. The same sentence carries three different messages.

### The power of stress

"I didn't say he made a mistake." – depending on stress it means different things:

- "**I** didn't say…" → someone else said it.
- "I **didn't** say…" → firm denial.
- "I didn't say **he** made a mistake." → someone else made it.

## Summary

- Verbal: words, sentences and register.
- Adapt your wording to the audience; be concrete and clear.
- Paraverbal: volume, pace, pitch, stress, pauses, articulation.
- The same sentence can send a completely different message depending on how it sounds.
