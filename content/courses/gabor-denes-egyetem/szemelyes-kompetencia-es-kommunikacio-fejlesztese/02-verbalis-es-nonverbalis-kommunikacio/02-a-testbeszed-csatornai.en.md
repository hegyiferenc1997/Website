## What you will learn

- the main channels of **nonverbal** communication,
- Edward T. Hall's **distance zones**,
- what **chronemics** (the message of time) means.

## What is nonverbal communication?

**Nonverbal** communication is everything we communicate **without words**. It mainly conveys emotions, attitudes and the relationship, and it is often less conscious than speech.

## The main channels

### Facial expression

The face is the most important expresser of emotions. According to Paul Ekman's research, the facial expressions of some **basic emotions** (joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust) are recognised across cultures.

### Gaze (eye contact)

- Appropriate eye contact: attention, sincerity, confidence.
- Avoiding gaze: uncertainty, embarrassment (but in some cultures a sign of respect!).
- Staring for too long: can feel threatening.

### Gestures

- **Illustrators**: accompany speech ("it was this big").
- **Emblems**: have a meaning of their own (nodding, thumbs up) – their meaning depends on culture.
- **Adaptors** (tension-relieving): clicking a pen, playing with hair – suggest nervousness.

### Posture

An **open** posture (arms not crossed, body turned towards the speaker) signals interest and acceptance; a **closed** one (crossed arms, turning away) signals distance. Don't draw conclusions from a single cue – the other person might simply be cold.

### Use of space (proxemics)

Edward T. Hall described four distance zones (the values come from North American observations and vary by culture):

| Zone         | Distance   | With whom?                     |
| ------------ | ---------- | ------------------------------ |
| **intimate** | 0–45 cm    | partner, family                |
| **personal** | 45–120 cm  | friends, close colleagues      |
| **social**   | 1.2–3.6 m  | co-workers, clients, strangers |
| **public**   | over 3.6 m | speaker and audience           |

### Touch (haptics)

Handshake, pat on the shoulder. Be careful at work: what one person finds friendly, another finds uncomfortable.

### Appearance and objects

Clothing, grooming, even your background in a video call send a message. For a job interview it is usually worth dressing one notch more formally than the company's everyday style.

### Chronemics (time)

Punctuality, lateness and response time are messages too. If you are always 10 minutes late for standup, you signal that the team's time matters less than yours.

## Summary

- Main channels: face, gaze, gesture, posture, space, touch, appearance, time.
- The expressions of basic emotions are largely universal; the meaning of gestures depends on culture.
- Hall's zones: intimate, personal, social, public.
- Don't draw far-reaching conclusions from a single cue.
