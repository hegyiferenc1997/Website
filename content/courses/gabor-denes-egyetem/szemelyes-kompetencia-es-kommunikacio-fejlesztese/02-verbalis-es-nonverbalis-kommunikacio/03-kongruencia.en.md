## What you will learn

- what **congruent** and **incongruent** messages are,
- what Mehrabian's famous experiment actually says,
- how to make up for nonverbal cues **online**.

## Congruence

A message is **congruent** when words, voice and body language all say the same thing. It is **incongruent** when they contradict each other.

> "No, I'm not angry," she says through clenched teeth, arms crossed. In such cases most of us believe the body language.

Incongruence creates distrust: the listener feels that "something is off" even if they can't put it into words.

## The "7–38–55 rule" – and what lies behind it

You often hear that communication is 7% words, 38% voice and 55% body language. This is a **misunderstanding**.

In the 1960s Albert Mehrabian studied single-word messages expressing **feelings or attitudes**, and only in **incongruent** situations (e.g. a kind word in a hostile tone). The result only says that when the channels contradict each other, voice and face weigh heavily **in judging the emotion**.

For a technical explanation or a specification, the content of the text obviously decides. If someone quotes the 7%, feel free to ask where it comes from.

## How to be congruent

- Only say what you really mean – fake enthusiasm shows.
- If something bothers you, say it **assertively** (see topic 3), don't just signal it with your body.
- Watch the other person: if they say yes but their body language is unsure, ask: "Looks like you still have a question – go ahead."

## Nonverbal cues online

In video calls and in writing many cues are lost or distorted.

| Situation   | What is lost?                | What can you do?                                              |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| video call  | eye contact, most of posture | look at the camera, good light, turn the camera on if you can |
| voice call  | every visual cue             | speak clearly, say when you agree ("got it, fine")            |
| chat, email | voice and body language      | unambiguous wording, emojis in moderation, check back         |

> It is hard to present to a silent audience with cameras off, because there is no feedback. As a presenter, ask for it actively: "Drop a 👍 in the chat if it's clear so far."

## Summary

- Congruent: words, voice and body say the same.
- When they contradict, we trust nonverbal cues in judging emotions.
- "7–38–55" only applies to incongruent, emotional messages; it is not a general rule.
- Online, consciously make up for lost cues.
