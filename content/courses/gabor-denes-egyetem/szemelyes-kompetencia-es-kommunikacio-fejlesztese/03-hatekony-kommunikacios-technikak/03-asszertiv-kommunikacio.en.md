## What you will learn

- the four basic **communication styles**,
- how to phrase an **I-message**,
- how to say **no** while keeping the relationship.

## Four styles

| Style                  | Basic stance                        | Example (a colleague asks for help at the last minute again)                    |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **passive**            | "Your needs matter more than mine." | "Sure, no problem…" (and you work until midnight)                               |
| **aggressive**         | "My needs matter more."             | "You always do this! Sort it out yourself!"                                     |
| **passive-aggressive** | hidden attack                       | "Sure, it's not like I have anything to do…" (and later you "forget")           |
| **assertive**          | "Both our needs matter."            | "I can help for an hour today until 5. Next time please tell me a day earlier." |

An **assertive** person stands up for their own interests and opinions while respecting the other's. It is not the same as rudeness, nor the same as submission.

## I-messages and you-messages

A **you-message** labels the other person, so it triggers defensiveness: "You never pay attention in meetings!"

An **I-message** (after Thomas Gordon) is about your own feelings and needs. Its structure:

```text
1. behaviour (fact):  "When you work on your laptop during the meeting,
2. feeling:           it bothers me,
3. effect / reason:   because I feel I have to repeat everything,
4. request:           could I ask you to close it during meetings?"
```

- Describe the **fact**, not your interpretation ("you work on your laptop", not "you don't care").
- The **request** should be concrete and achievable.

## Saying no

If you say yes to everything, your own tasks slip and in the end nobody is satisfied. An assertive no:

1. **Short** and clear: "I can't take this on right now."
2. Optionally a short **reason** (not an excuse): "I have to hand in my own module tomorrow."
3. If you can, an **alternative**: "I'm happy to look at it on Thursday" or "Ask Dora, she knows that part."

> The "broken record" technique: if the other person pushes, calmly repeat your no with the same sentence – you don't have to invent new reasons again and again.

## Summary

- Passive, aggressive, passive-aggressive, assertive – the assertive style respects both parties' needs.
- I-message: fact → feeling → effect → request.
- Saying no: briefly, with or without a reason, preferably with an alternative.
