## What you will learn

- the difference between **hearing** and **listening**,
- the techniques of **active listening**,
- typical **listening mistakes**.

## Hearing or listening?

**Hearing** is a physical process: sound reaches our ears. **Listening** is mental work: we pay attention, interpret and try to understand the other person.

**Active listening** (after Carl Rogers) means not just being quiet, but also **signalling** that we are paying attention and **checking** that we understood correctly.

## Techniques of active listening

| Technique               | What you do                             | Example                                                    |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **showing attention**   | eye contact, nodding, "uh-huh", "I see" | –                                                          |
| **paraphrasing**        | repeat the content in your own words    | "So the test environment is slow, so you couldn't run it." |
| **reflecting feelings** | name the other person's feeling         | "Sounds like that was pretty frustrating."                 |
| **clarifying question** | ask about the vague part                | "What do you mean by it 'sometimes' crashes?"              |
| **summarising**         | sum up the essentials at the end        | "We agreed on three things: …"                             |
| **silence**             | give the other person time to think     | –                                                          |

> Paraphrasing is not word-for-word repetition ("parroting") but feedback on your understanding. If you got it wrong, the other person corrects you – and that's exactly the point.

## Typical listening mistakes

- **Interrupting**: you're already composing your answer before the other person has finished.
- **Instant solutions**: someone just wants to share a problem and you jump straight to advice.
- **"Me too" stories**: steering the conversation to your own experience.
- **Judging**: "How did you not notice that?"
- **Selective listening**: only hearing what supports your opinion.
- **Multitasking**: reading emails meanwhile.

## Why bother?

- Fewer misunderstandings, less rework.
- The other person feels **understood**, so they become more open to your arguments too.
- It works for debugging as well: whoever listens carefully to a user's complaint often discovers the cause of the bug.

## Summary

- Listening is active mental work.
- Techniques: showing attention, paraphrasing, reflecting feelings, clarifying questions, summarising, silence.
- Avoid interrupting, instant advice, judging and multitasking.
