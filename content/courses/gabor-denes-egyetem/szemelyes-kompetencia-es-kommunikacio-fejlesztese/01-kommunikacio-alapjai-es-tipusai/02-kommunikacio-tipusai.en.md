## What you will learn

- the **levels** of communication by number of participants,
- the difference between **formal** and **informal** communication,
- what **synchronous** and **asynchronous** communication are,
- the **directions** of organisational communication.

## By number of participants

| Level              | Participants             | Example                                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| **intrapersonal**  | with yourself            | inner monologue before a decision         |
| **interpersonal**  | two people               | a one-on-one (1:1) with your mentor       |
| **group**          | 3–15 people              | daily standup, team meeting               |
| **organisational** | a company or institution | internal newsletter, all-hands meeting    |
| **mass**           | large, unknown audience  | blog post, conference talk, YouTube video |

The more participants there are, the harder direct feedback becomes and the more important careful preparation of the message is.

## Formal and informal

- **Formal**: travels along channels designated and regulated by the organisation (official email, minutes, a ticket in the issue tracker). Its advantage is traceability.
- **Informal**: spontaneous and casual (a chat by the coffee machine, a private message). Fast and trust-building, but rumours can spread through it too.

> A good team needs both: decisions should be recorded formally, while informal channels keep relationships alive.

## Synchronous and asynchronous

- **Synchronous**: the parties are present at the same time (face-to-face, phone, video call). Fast feedback, but everyone has to be available at once.
- **Asynchronous**: the reply comes later (email, ticket, wiki, chat message). Works across time zones and leaves time to think the answer through.

Remote software teams often work **"async first"** on purpose: whatever they can, they handle in writing, and they only call meetings for topics that truly require thinking together.

## Directions in an organisation

- **Downward** (from manager to report): goals, tasks, decisions.
- **Upward** (from report to manager): reports, problems, suggestions.
- **Lateral (horizontal)**: between colleagues or teams at the same level, e.g. the backend and frontend teams coordinating.
- **Diagonal**: between people at different levels and in different areas, e.g. a junior developer talking directly to the marketing director.

## Verbal, nonverbal, written

We can also group by channel: **spoken**, **written**, **nonverbal** (body language) and **visual** (charts, diagrams). Topics 2 and 6 cover these in detail.

## Summary

- Levels: intrapersonal, interpersonal, group, organisational, mass.
- Formal communication is traceable; informal is fast and builds trust.
- Synchronous: at the same time; asynchronous: with a delay.
- Organisational directions: downward, upward, lateral, diagonal.
