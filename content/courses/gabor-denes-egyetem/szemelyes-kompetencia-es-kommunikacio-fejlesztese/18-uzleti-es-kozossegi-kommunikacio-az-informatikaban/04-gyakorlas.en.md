## Review questions for the topic

<details>
<summary>1. Write a user story with acceptance criteria for a dorm washing-machine booking app!</summary>

"As a dorm resident, I want to see when the washing machine is free and book a slot, so that I don't have to go down to check." Acceptance criteria: slots for today and the next 6 days are visible; a slot can only be booked by one person; bookings can be cancelled; a notification arrives 15 minutes before the booking.

</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Why should you update the status page during an outage even if there's no new information?</summary>

Because users interpret silence as nobody working on the problem (Watzlawick: one cannot not communicate). A "we're still working on it, next update in 30 minutes" message reduces uncertainty and complaints.

</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Write a code review comment about a function being too long (120 lines)!</summary>

"This function currently does three things (reading, validating, saving) and is about 120 lines. How about splitting it into three smaller functions? That would make it easier to test and read. (Not blocking – if it's urgent now, it can go into a separate issue.)"

</details>

<details>
<summary>4. Which three of the course's topics help most in a job interview, and why?</summary>

For example: presentations and delivery (a confident introduction, the STAR method), personal branding (CV, GitHub, LinkedIn), and effective communication techniques (active listening, questioning, assertiveness). But you could equally argue for nonverbal communication or self-reflection – what matters is the reasoning.

</details>

## The course's final worksheet

> A four-person student team (Anna, Ben, Cecilia and David) is building a web app for placing seasonal workers for a small business, in 10 weeks. Follow the project!

```quiz required
? At the first meeting the client says: "Make it modern and simple." Cecilia asks: "What does modern mean to you? Could you show me a site you like?" Which noise is she trying to reduce, and with which technique?
- physical noise, by interrupting
+ semantic noise, with a clarifying question
- physiological noise, with silence
- psychological noise, by judging
! "Modern" means something different to everyone (topics 1 and 3).

? In the first two weeks the team argues a lot about the framework. Which stage of Tuckman's model are they in?
- forming
+ storming
- norming
- performing
! Storming (topic 11).

? David keeps coming up with new ideas but doesn't finish anything. Which Belbin role characterises him?
+ plant
- completer finisher
- implementer
- coordinator
! Plant (topic 10).

? The client has budgeted at most 800 thousand HUF for the project; the team wants at least 500 thousand. How many thousand HUF wide is the ZOPA?
= 300
! 800 − 500 = 300 (topic 7).

? When discussing the price, the client opens with "another team would do it for 200 thousand". What is this in negotiation terms?
- the team's BATNA
+ anchoring (and a reference to the client's possible BATNA)
- an objective criterion
- the ZOPA
! The first number sets the starting point (topic 7).

? How many two-person communication channels are there in the 4-person team?
= 6
! 4 · 3 / 2 = 6 (topic 11).

? In a code review Ben writes to Anna: "This code is horrible, you can't program." What is the main problem?
- it's too short
+ it attacks the person instead of the code (a you-message, ad hominem)
- it's too polite
- he didn't write it in English
! About the code, specifically, with reasons (topics 3, 5 and 18).

? Cecilia gives Ben SBI feedback. Which one is good?
- "Ben, you're always hurtful."
+ "In yesterday's code review (S) you wrote to Anna that she can't program (B); afterwards she didn't speak at the meeting (I). Let's talk about how we write comments."
- "Good job, Ben!"
- "Something's wrong with you."
! Situation, behaviour, impact (topic 10).

? In week 6 the team realises they won't finish the full feature set. What should they do?
- stay quiet until the last day
+ tell the client in time and offer options (e.g. postponing one feature)
- add two new team members in week 9
- secretly leave out features
! Early warning + options; Brooks's law (topics 11 and 18).

? Anna plans 8 pomodoros for the payment module. How many minutes of work is that (without breaks)?
= 200
! 8 · 25 = 200 minutes (topic 14).

? On handover day, login doesn't work between 14:05 and 14:50. Which is a good message to the client at 14:10?
- write nothing until it's fixed
+ "We're aware that login isn't working right now and are investigating the cause; we'll update you at 14:30."
- "It must be your server's fault."
- "Ben broke it."
! Early, honest, specific, with the time of the next update (topic 18).

? How many minutes did the login problem last?
= 45
! 14:50 − 14:05 = 45 minutes.

? After the project the team sits down and discusses what went well, what didn't, and what they would do differently. What is this?
- sprint planning
+ a retrospective (and a blameless review at the same time)
- a daily standup
- a negotiation
! Topics 11 and 18.

? Later, in her learning journal, Anna works through handover day with Gibbs' cycle. In which step does she write "next time we'll freeze the code one day before handover"?
- description
- feelings
- analysis
+ action plan
! Topic 16.

? Ben adds the project to his portfolio. Which description is good?
- "I developed a complete web application on my own."
+ "In a four-person team I was responsible for the backend and the database; we delivered the project to a small business in 10 weeks."
- "I took part in something."
- he leaves it out because it was teamwork
! Exactly his own part (topic 12).
```
