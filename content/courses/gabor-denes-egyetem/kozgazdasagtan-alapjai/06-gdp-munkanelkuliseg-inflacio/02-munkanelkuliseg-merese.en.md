## What you will learn

- how statistics divide the **population**,
- who counts as **unemployed** under the ILO definition,
- how to calculate the **unemployment**, **activity** and **employment rates**,
- why the unemployment rate alone can be misleading.

## Dividing the population

Based on the **labour force survey** (a large, regular household questionnaire), the Hungarian Central Statistical Office (KSH) divides the population **aged 15–74** into three groups:

```text
                population aged 15–74
              ┌───────────┴───────────┐
     economically active          inactive
       (labour force)      (students, pensioners,
     ┌──────┴──────┐        people on childcare leave,
  employed      unemployed     not looking for work)
```

- **Employed:** worked at least **1 hour** for pay in the reference week (or was only temporarily absent, e.g. on holiday).
- **Unemployed** (ILO definition): **has no job**, **actively looked** for work in the past **4 weeks**, and **could start work within 2 weeks**.
- **Inactive:** neither working nor looking for work – e.g. most full-time university students, pensioners.

## The rates

$$ \text{unemployment rate} = \frac{\text{unemployed}}{\text{active}} \cdot 100 $$

$$ \text{activity rate} = \frac{\text{active}}{\text{population aged 15–74}} \cdot 100 $$

$$ \text{employment rate} = \frac{\text{employed}}{\text{population aged 15–74}} \cdot 100 $$

Note: the denominator of the unemployment rate is the number of **active** people, not the whole population!

**Example** (thousands): population aged 15–74 is 7,000, employed 4,750, unemployed 250.

- active: $4750 + 250 = 5000$,
- unemployment rate: $250 / 5000 = 5\%$,
- activity rate: $5000 / 7000 \approx 71.4\%$,
- employment rate: $4750 / 7000 \approx 67.9\%$.

In Hungary the unemployment rate has been **around 4–5%** in recent years, which is low by EU standards.

## Registered unemployed

Besides the labour force survey there is also the number of **registered jobseekers** (those who registered with the government office). The two are not the same: some look for work but do not register, others are registered but do not actively search. For international comparisons the ILO figure is the right one.

## Why is the rate alone misleading?

- **Discouraged workers:** those who gave up searching move to the **inactive** group – the unemployment rate **falls**, even though nobody got a job.
- **Underemployment:** someone working 10 hours a week who wants more counts as employed.
- **Public works schemes:** public works participants count as employed, even though they often do not work on the market labour market.
- **Hidden unemployment:** the work of people in oversized organisations is not really needed.

That is why the **employment rate** often shows the state of the labour market better. Types and causes of unemployment are covered in topic 8.

## Summary

- Aged 15–74: employed + unemployed = active; the rest are inactive.
- ILO unemployed: no job, searched in the past 4 weeks, can start within 2 weeks.
- Unemployment rate = unemployed / active.
- Discouraged workers and public works schemes distort the rate.
