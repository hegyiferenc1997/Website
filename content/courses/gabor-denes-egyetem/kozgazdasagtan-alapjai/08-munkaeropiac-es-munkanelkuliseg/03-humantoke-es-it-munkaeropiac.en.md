## What you will learn

- what **human capital** is and how to calculate the **return** on education,
- what **signalling theory** says about degrees,
- what characterises the **labour market for IT professionals**,
- how **remote work**, the **platform economy** and **automation** are shaping work.

## Human capital

**Human capital** is the knowledge, skills, experience and health accumulated in people that raise their productivity. Like physical capital, it can be **invested in** – through study, practice and training.

### Is it worth going to university?

The **costs** of university: fees, books, equipment **and** the opportunity cost – forgone earnings (topic 1). The **benefits**: higher lifetime earnings with a degree, more interesting work, lower risk of unemployment.

**Simplified example:**

- net wages forgone over 3.5 years: 3 million HUF a year → about **10.5 million HUF**,
- other costs: about **1.5 million HUF** → **12 million HUF** in total,
- with the degree you earn **2 million HUF** more a year.

Simple payback period: $12 / 2 = 6$ years. After that, over a whole career (40 years) the extra earnings repay the investment many times over. (More precisely, we should use present values – topic 5.)

Studies show a significant **graduate wage premium** in Hungary too, and much lower unemployment among graduates.

### Signalling theory

According to Michael Spence, a degree not only provides knowledge but is also a **signal** to employers: someone who could complete it is probably persistent, smart and organised. That is why a diploma is valuable even if not every subject is needed in the job. In IT a **portfolio** is also a strong signal: GitHub projects, competition results, practical work.

## The labour market for IT professionals

- **Strong demand:** almost every industry is digitalising; the EU has had a shortage of ICT specialists for years, and has set a target of 20 million ICT specialists by 2030 (the "Digital Decade" programme).
- **High pay and wide pay dispersion:** the gap between junior and senior pay is large – experience (accumulated human capital) is worth a lot.
- **Fast-ageing knowledge:** a framework can become outdated in a few years → **lifelong learning**. The fundamentals (algorithms, mathematics, networks, systems thinking) last longer than any one tool.
- **Cyclicality:** the tech sector is also sensitive to the business cycle; in 2022–2023, after interest rate rises, several large companies made significant layoffs.

## Remote work and the global labour market

Since the 2020 pandemic, **remote** and **hybrid work** have become widespread. Economic consequences:

- the labour market becomes **global**: a developer in Debrecen can work for a company in Amsterdam – more opportunities, but more competition too,
- wages between regions **converge**,
- housing and commuting costs may fall, but teamwork and mentoring become harder.

## Platform work

In the **platform economy** (food delivery and ride-hailing apps, freelancing platforms) work is allocated by an algorithm. Its advantage is flexibility; its drawbacks are uncertain income and weak social protection. In 2024 the EU adopted a directive to improve the situation of platform workers (e.g. a presumption of employment, transparency of algorithmic management).

## Automation and artificial intelligence

Technology replaces **tasks**, not necessarily whole jobs:

- routine tasks (data entry, simple code generation, customer service replies) can be automated,
- new jobs emerge (data engineer, AI safety specialist, cloud architect),
- complementary skills gain value: problem solving, system design, communication, domain knowledge.

Historically, technological progress has **not** caused mass unemployment in the long run, but many people have temporarily had to **switch** – this causes structural unemployment if there is no retraining. More on this in topic 12.

## Summary

- Human capital is an investment: its cost is fees and forgone earnings, its benefit higher lifetime earnings.
- A degree is also a signal; in IT a portfolio is a strong signal too.
- IT labour market: strong demand, high pay, fast-ageing knowledge, cyclicality.
- Remote work: global competition and opportunities; platform work: flexibility but uncertainty.
- Automation replaces tasks and creates new jobs.
