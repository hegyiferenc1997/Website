## What you will learn

- what the **two-tier banking system** is,
- what a **commercial bank** does,
- how banks **create money** by lending,
- what the **reserve ratio** and the **money multiplier** are,
- why a **bank run** is dangerous and what **deposit insurance** protects.

## The two-tier banking system

| Tier     | Who?                                  | Role                                                                |
| -------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1st tier | **central bank** (in Hungary the MNB) | issuing cash, monetary policy, the banks' bank, supervision         |
| 2nd tier | **commercial banks**                  | taking deposits, lending, payment services for households and firms |

In Hungary the two-tier banking system was re-established in 1987 (before that the MNB was both the central bank and the commercial bank).

## What does a commercial bank do?

A bank is a **financial intermediary**: it connects savers and borrowers.

- It pays **deposit interest** on deposits and charges **lending interest** on loans.
- The difference between the two (the **interest margin**) and fees provide its income.
- **Maturity transformation:** it makes long-term loans (e.g. a 20-year mortgage) from short-term deposits – this is useful, but risky.

## How do banks create money?

The surprising truth: **when a bank makes a loan, new money is created**. The bank does not "hand over" depositors' money; it credits a new deposit to the borrower's account.

**Simplified example** with a 10% reserve ratio:

1. Anna deposits 1,000,000 HUF in cash in bank A.
2. Bank A keeps 100,000 HUF in reserve and lends **900,000 HUF** to Ben.
3. Ben uses it to pay a software developer, who deposits it in bank B. Bank B keeps 90,000 HUF in reserve and lends out **810,000 HUF** again.
4. And so on…

In the end total deposits are: $1\,000\,000 + 900\,000 + 810\,000 + \dots = 10\,000\,000$ HUF.

## The money multiplier

$$ \text{money multiplier} = \frac{1}{\text{reserve ratio}} $$

With a 10% reserve ratio the multiplier is **10**: 1 HUF of new base money can in theory become 10 HUF of bank money.

In reality money creation is limited not mainly by the reserve ratio but by **loan demand** (someone has to borrow), banks' **capital** and **willingness to take risk**, and the central bank's **interest rate policy**. When a loan is repaid, the money disappears.

## Bank runs and deposit insurance

Because banks lend out most of their deposits, they **cannot pay everyone** at once. If depositors lose confidence and want to withdraw their money at the same time (a **bank run**), even an otherwise healthy bank can fail – and the panic can spread to other banks.

Safety nets:

- **Deposit insurance:** in the EU deposits are guaranteed up to **100,000 euros** per bank per depositor (in Hungary the National Deposit Insurance Fund, OBA, pays out).
- **Lender of last resort:** in a crisis the central bank provides liquidity (short-term loans) to solvent banks.
- **Regulation and supervision:** capital requirements (Basel III), stress tests – more on this in topic 11.

**Example:** in March 2023 California's Silicon Valley Bank, a favourite of start-ups and tech companies, collapsed within days when customers tried to withdraw more than 40 billion dollars in a single day – thanks to social media and mobile banking the run happened at unprecedented speed.

## Summary

- Two-tier banking system: central bank + commercial banks.
- Banks are financial intermediaries; their income is the interest margin and fees.
- Lending creates new bank money; repayment destroys it.
- Money multiplier = 1 / reserve ratio (a theoretical upper limit).
- Against bank runs: deposit insurance (100,000 euros), lender of last resort, regulation.
