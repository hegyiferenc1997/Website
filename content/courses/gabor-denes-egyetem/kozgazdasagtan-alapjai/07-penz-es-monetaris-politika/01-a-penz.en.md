## What you will learn

- the **four functions** of money,
- how the **forms** of money evolved from commodity money to digital money,
- whether **crypto assets** can be considered money,
- what **monetary aggregates** (M1, M2, M3) are,
- what the **quantity equation** says.

## Why does money exist?

Without money there would be **barter**: a programmer who wants bread would have to find a baker who happens to want a website. This is the problem of the "double coincidence of wants". Money solves it.

## The functions of money

| Function                         | Meaning                                         | Example                                       |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **medium of exchange**           | everyone accepts it as payment                  | you pay in the canteen                        |
| **unit of account**              | all prices can be expressed in the same unit    | the laptop is 300,000 HUF, the coffee 900 HUF |
| **store of value**               | purchasing power can be carried into the future | you save for the summer                       |
| **standard of deferred payment** | debts can be fixed in money                     | repaying a loan                               |

## Forms of money

1. **Commodity money:** a good that is valuable in itself (salt, shells, gold, silver coins).
2. **Backed paper money:** banknotes convertible into gold (the gold standard – it ended in the 20th century; the dollar's convertibility into gold ended in 1971).
3. **Fiat money** (what we have today): there is no gold behind it; its value comes from **trust** and its status as **legal tender** – the state accepts it for taxes, and the central bank protects its value.
4. **Cash:** banknotes and coins (in Hungary issued by the MNB).
5. **Bank money:** deposits in bank accounts – the **vast majority** of the money supply. Card and instant payments (in Hungary the **instant payment system** has made transfers within 5 seconds possible since 2020).

### Crypto assets

Bitcoin and similar coins are decentralised, blockchain-based digital assets. As money, however, they are **weak**: their price is very volatile (a poor store of value and unit of account), few places accept them, and there is no central bank behind them. That is why the literature prefers to call them **crypto assets**. In the EU the **MiCA** regulation has governed crypto-asset service providers since 2024.

**Stablecoins** have a value pegged to a traditional currency (e.g. the dollar) – they are more stable, but depend on the quality of their backing.

### Central bank digital currency (CBDC)

Many central banks are studying **central bank digital currency**: it is like cash, only electronic, and a direct liability of the central bank. The European Central Bank is preparing the **digital euro**.

## Monetary aggregates

The money supply is measured with different definitions according to **liquidity** (how quickly it can be spent):

- **M0 (base money):** cash + banks' reserves at the central bank,
- **M1:** cash in circulation + **overnight deposits** (current accounts),
- **M2:** M1 + short-term deposits,
- **M3:** M2 + other near-money assets (e.g. some money market fund shares).

## The quantity equation

$$ M \cdot V = P \cdot Y $$

- $M$: money supply, $V$: the **velocity** of money (how many times a forint changes hands in a year),
- $P$: price level, $Y$: real output (real GDP).

If $V$ and $Y$ are roughly constant, growth in the money supply leads to a **rise in the price level**. In the long run this explains big inflations well (the pengő hyperinflation was also caused by printing money).

## Summary

- Functions: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, standard of deferred payment.
- Today we have fiat money; most money is bank money.
- Crypto assets perform the functions of money poorly; a CBDC is digital central bank money.
- M1 ⊂ M2 ⊂ M3, by liquidity.
- $MV = PY$: too much money leads to inflation in the long run.
