## What you will learn

- who the **main actors** of the economy are and what they do,
- what the **product market** and the **factor market** are,
- how the **circular flow of income** works,
- what **leakages** and **injections** are.

## Actors of the economy

| Actor                      | What do they give?                                                  | What do they get?                    |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| households                 | labour, capital (savings), consumer spending                        | wages, interest, dividends, products |
| firms                      | goods, services, wages                                              | sales revenue, factors of production |
| government                 | public services, transfers (pensions, benefits), public procurement | taxes, contributions                 |
| rest of the world          | imports, foreign investment                                         | export revenue                       |
| (financial intermediaries) | loans                                                               | savings                              |

A **household** can be a university student living alone or a family: a unit managing a shared income.

## Two markets

- **Product market** (goods and services market): firms sell their products to households here – laptops, bread, mobile internet.
- **Factor market**: firms buy factors of production from households here – the most important is the **labour market** (topic 8), but the capital and property markets belong here too.

## The simple circular flow

In the simplest model there are only households and firms:

```text
            ┌──── consumer spending (HUF) ─────┐
            │                                   ▼
   ┌─────────────┐   ◄── products ───   ┌─────────────┐
   │ HOUSEHOLDS  │                      │    FIRMS    │
   └─────────────┘   ── labour, capital ►└─────────────┘
            ▲                                   │
            └── wages, interest, profit (HUF) ──┘
```

There are flows in two directions:

- the **real flow** (goods, labour) in one direction,
- the **money flow** (spending, income) in the opposite direction.

An important consequence: **one actor's spending is another actor's income**. So the total output, total income and total spending of the economy are in principle equal – the three ways of measuring GDP rest on this (topic 6).

## Leakages and injections

In reality, part of income does not flow straight back to firms:

| Leakage     | Injection               |
| ----------- | ----------------------- |
| saving (S)  | investment (I)          |
| taxes (T)   | government spending (G) |
| imports (M) | exports (X)             |

- If **leakages > injections**, the flow "shrinks" (demand and output fall).
- If **injections > leakages**, the flow expands.

**Example:** a Hungarian software company sells a service to a German client – this is an **export**, money flows into the Hungarian circular flow. If you buy an American game, that is an **import** – part of the money leaks out.

## The role of the financial system

Banks and capital markets connect **savers** (mainly households) and **borrowers** (business investment, home buyers, the government). In this way savings become investment – more on this in topic 7.

## Summary

- Actors: households, firms, government, rest of the world (and financial intermediaries).
- Products change hands on the product market, factors of production on the factor market.
- In the circular flow the real and money flows go in opposite directions; one's spending is another's income.
- Leakages: S, T, M; injections: I, G, X.
