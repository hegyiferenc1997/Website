## What you will learn

- what **microeconomics** and **macroeconomics** study,
- what questions each of them asks,
- how the two levels are connected,
- which topic of this course belongs to which field.

## The two main fields

| Aspect        | Microeconomics                                        | Macroeconomics                                                     |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| unit studied  | individual households, firms, markets                 | the whole national economy, the world economy                      |
| main question | how are the price and quantity of a product set?      | how large are total output, the price level, employment?           |
| key concepts  | demand, supply, price, cost, profit, market structure | GDP, inflation, unemployment, interest rate, exchange rate, budget |
| actors        | consumer, firm, one industry                          | "households", "firms", government, central bank, rest of the world |
| example       | Why did graphics cards get more expensive?            | Why did the price level rise in Hungary in 2022–2023?              |

> The forest and the trees: microeconomics looks at the trees one by one, macroeconomics at the whole forest.

## Typical microeconomic questions

- How does demand react if a streaming service raises its subscription price by 20%?
- How many developers should a company hire?
- Why is a monopoly's price higher than a competitive market's?
- How does a student decide how to spend their pocket money?

In this course mainly **topics 3 and 4** are microeconomic.

## Typical macroeconomic questions

- Why do some countries' economies grow faster than others'?
- What causes inflation, and how can it be curbed?
- Why is there unemployment, and what can the government do about it?
- How does a central bank rate hike affect loans and the exchange rate of the forint?

**Topics 6–11** are mainly macroeconomic; topic 5 (budget) and topic 12 (IT) touch both levels.

## How are the two connected?

Macroeconomic phenomena are **built up from the sum of micro-level decisions**, and they feed back into them:

```text
 millions of individual decisions (buying, working, investing)
            │ add up
            ▼
 macro data: GDP, inflation, unemployment
            │ feed back (interest rates, taxes, expectations)
            ▼
 new individual decisions
```

**Beware – the fallacy of composition:** what is good for one actor is not necessarily good for everyone together. If one household saves more during a crisis, it gains security. If **everyone** saves more at the same time, demand falls, firms produce less, and incomes may fall too (**paradox of thrift**).

## Summary

- Microeconomics: decisions of individual actors and markets (price, quantity, cost).
- Macroeconomics: the national economy as a whole (GDP, inflation, unemployment, interest rate).
- Macro is the sum of micro decisions and feeds back into them.
- Fallacy of composition: what is good for one actor is not always good for all.
