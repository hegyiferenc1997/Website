## What you will learn

- the **three basic economic questions**,
- how **traditional**, **command** and **market** economies answer them,
- why almost every country today is a **mixed economy**,
- what the **invisible hand** and **market failure** are.

## The three basic questions

Because of scarcity, every society has to decide:

1. **What** to produce and how much? (smartphones or bread, a hospital or a stadium?)
2. **How** to produce? (with many workers or with robots, from coal or solar power?)
3. **For whom** to produce? (how are income and goods distributed?)

An **economic system** is the set of rules and institutions that answers these questions.

## Types of economic systems

| System      | Who decides?            | What coordinates?         | Example                                   |
| ----------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| traditional | custom, the community   | tradition                 | self-sufficient village communities       |
| command     | central planner (state) | orders, the plan          | Soviet Union, North Korea today           |
| market      | households and firms    | prices, supply and demand | nowhere in pure form, a theoretical model |
| mixed       | market + state together | prices and state rules    | Hungary, EU countries, USA                |

### Command economy

A central office draws up a plan: which factory produces what, how much and at what price.

- **Advantage:** resources can quickly be redirected to a priority goal (e.g. heavy industry).
- **Disadvantages:** the planner cannot know the needs of millions (**information problem**), **incentives** are weak (extra work does not pay), shortages appear (queues), innovation is slow.

Hungary had a command economy between 1949 and 1989; from 1968 the "New Economic Mechanism" introduced some market elements.

### Market economy

Decisions are made by **privately owned** firms and by households; **prices** do the coordination.

- If a product is scarce, its price rises → producers find it worthwhile to make more, buyers buy less.
- Adam Smith (1776) called this the **invisible hand**: actors pursuing their own interest also serve the common good through prices – without intending to.

**Advantages:** efficient use of resources, strong incentives, fast innovation, freedom of choice.

## Market failures – when the invisible hand is not enough

The market does not always deliver good outcomes. The main cases of **market failure**:

- **Externality**: a decision affects others, but the price does not reflect this. Negative: pollution; positive: open-source software, vaccination.
- **Public goods**: nobody can be excluded from using them, and one person's use does not reduce another's (street lighting, national defence). The market under-supplies them because everyone would "free ride".
- **Market power**: with a monopoly the price is higher and the quantity lower (topic 4).
- **Asymmetric information**: one side knows more (used-car market, insurance).
- **Inequality**: the market does not guarantee a fair distribution of income.

## Mixed economy

That is why almost every country today is a **mixed economy**: most goods are produced by the market, but the state:

- **regulates** (competition law, consumer protection, data protection – e.g. GDPR),
- provides **public goods** (education, healthcare, roads, public safety),
- **redistributes** income (taxes, pensions, family benefits),
- **stabilises** the economy (budget – topic 5, central bank – topic 7).

Today's debate is not "market or state" but **how much** state and **what kind** of rules we need.

## Summary

- Basic questions: what, how, for whom to produce.
- Command economy: central orders; problems with information and incentives.
- Market economy: prices coordinate (invisible hand), but market failures exist.
- Market failures: externalities, public goods, market power, asymmetric information.
- Mixed economies are the norm today.
