## What you will learn

- the main **revenues** and **expenditures** of the government budget,
- what the **deficit** and **public debt** are,
- what **expansionary** and **contractionary** fiscal policy mean,
- what **automatic stabilisers** are,
- how the **multiplier** works.

## The budget

The **government budget** is the state's financial plan for a year: how much revenue it expects and how much it spends on what. In Hungary it is adopted by Parliament as a law.

### Main revenues

| Revenue           | Hungarian example (2026)                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| consumption taxes | **VAT** (standard rate 27%, one of the highest in the EU), excise duties (fuel, tobacco, alcohol)          |
| income taxes      | **personal income tax** (flat, 15%; people under 25 are exempt up to the average wage), corporate tax (9%) |
| contributions     | social security contribution, social contribution tax                                                      |
| other             | EU funds, income from state assets, duties                                                                 |

### Main expenditures

- **social spending**: pensions, family benefits, welfare payments (these are **transfers**: payments with nothing given in return),
- **public services**: healthcare, education, public safety, defence,
- **investment**: roads, railways, digital infrastructure,
- **interest** on public debt.

## Deficit and public debt

- **Budget balance** = revenues − expenditures.
- If negative: a **deficit**; if positive: a **surplus**.
- **Primary balance**: the balance excluding interest payments – it shows whether "current" spending is covered by revenues.
- **Public debt**: the **accumulated** sum of past deficits. The deficit is a "flow" over a year, the debt is a "stock" at a point in time – like water flowing into a bathtub and the water in the tub.

Countries are compared **relative to GDP**. Under EU rules (the Maastricht criteria) the deficit may be at most **3%** of GDP and public debt at most **60%** of GDP. Hungary's debt has been around 70–75% of GDP in recent years.

## Fiscal policy

**Fiscal policy** is the government's decisions on taxes and spending to influence the economy.

|        | Expansionary                                  | Contractionary                                  |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| tool   | more spending, tax cuts                       | less spending, tax rises                        |
| when?  | recession, high unemployment                  | overheating economy, high inflation, high debt  |
| effect | demand and output rise, but the deficit grows | demand and inflation fall, the balance improves |

Its dangers: **time lags** (by the time the decision takes effect, the situation may have changed), and **crowding out** – if the government borrows a lot, interest rates rise and private investment falls.

## Automatic stabilisers

Some elements dampen the cycle **without any separate decision**:

- in a recession tax revenue falls (incomes are lower) and unemployment benefits rise → the budget automatically becomes "expansionary",
- in a boom the opposite happens.

## The multiplier

If the government spends 100 billion HUF (e.g. on renovating a railway), this becomes income for the construction firms' workers. They spend part of it, which becomes new income – and so on. The final effect is **larger** than the original spending.

- **Marginal propensity to consume (MPC):** how much of an extra 1 HUF of income is spent.
- **Simple multiplier:** $k = \dfrac{1}{1 - MPC}$.

If $MPC = 0.75$: $k = 1/0.25 = 4$ → 100 billion HUF of spending in theory raises income by 400 billion HUF. In reality (because of taxes, imports and saving) it is much smaller, often around 1.

## Summary

- Revenues: VAT, income tax, corporate tax, contributions; expenditures: transfers, public services, investment, interest.
- Deficit = the gap between a year's spending and revenue; debt = accumulated deficits.
- Expansionary policy: more spending, lower taxes; contractionary: the opposite.
- Automatic stabilisers: taxes and benefits dampen the cycle.
- Multiplier: $1/(1 - MPC)$.
