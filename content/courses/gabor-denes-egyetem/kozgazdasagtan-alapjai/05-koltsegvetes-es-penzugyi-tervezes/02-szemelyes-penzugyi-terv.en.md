## What you will learn

- how to make a **monthly budget**,
- the difference between **fixed** and **variable**, **needs** and **wants** expenses,
- how the **50/30/20 rule** works,
- why you need an **emergency fund**,
- how to set **financial goals**.

## Why do you need a budget?

The principle is the same as for the government: you **plan** income and expenses **in advance** and later **compare them with what actually happened**. Those who do not plan "run out of money" by the end of the month and do not know where it went.

## Step 1: income

Typical income for a university student: scholarship, student job or internship pay, family support. Always calculate with the **net** amount (after taxes and contributions). Good news: in Hungary people under 25 pay no personal income tax up to the average wage, so the gap between their gross and net pay is smaller.

## Step 2: tracking expenses

For a month, write down **every** expense (banking app, spreadsheet or a small program of your own). It is worth grouping expenses:

|           | Fixed (same every month)         | Variable                                   |
| --------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Needs** | rent, transport pass, phone plan | groceries, utilities                       |
| **Wants** | streaming subscriptions, gym     | restaurants, entertainment, clothes, games |

Many small items (daily coffee, small subscriptions) can add up to a surprisingly large amount each month: a 900 HUF coffee a day is about 27,000 HUF a month.

## Step 3: the plan – the 50/30/20 rule

A simple starting point for splitting net income:

- **50%** needs (housing, food, transport),
- **30%** wants (entertainment, hobbies),
- **20%** saving and debt repayment.

**Example:** with a net income of 250,000 HUF: 125,000 HUF needs, 75,000 HUF wants, 50,000 HUF savings.

This is not set in stone – in Budapest renting a flat alone can take half of your income. The point is to decide **consciously** and make saving a "fixed item": **pay yourself first** (transfer the savings to a savings account as soon as you are paid).

## Emergency fund

Unexpected expenses always come: the laptop breaks, you need a dentist, you lose your job. An **emergency fund** is a quickly accessible amount, typically equal to **3–6 months** of necessary expenses.

- Without it, an unexpected expense forces you into expensive credit (credit card, store credit).
- Keep it somewhere you can access quickly but will not spend by accident (a separate savings account).

## Financial goals

A good goal is **SMART**: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

- Bad: "I want to save more."
- Good: "In 12 months I will save 360,000 HUF for a new laptop, 30,000 HUF a month."

Short term (laptop, holiday), medium term (car, a home deposit), long term (home, pension) – for long-term goals **compound interest** (next lesson) is your best friend.

## Summary

- Budget: plan income, track expenses, compare plan and actual.
- Expenses: fixed / variable, needs / wants.
- 50/30/20: needs / wants / savings – "pay yourself first".
- Emergency fund: 3–6 months of necessary expenses.
- Goals should be SMART.
