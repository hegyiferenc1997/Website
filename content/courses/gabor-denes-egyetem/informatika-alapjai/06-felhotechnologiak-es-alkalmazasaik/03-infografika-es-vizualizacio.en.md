## What you will learn

- which **chart type** is for what,
- what an **infographic** is and how to make a good one,
- which **cloud tools** you can visualise with, and how **not** to mislead with a chart.

## Why visualise?

It is hard to see the point in a table of a thousand numbers; a good chart shows the **trend**, the **proportion** or the **outlier** at a glance. Visualisation turns data into **information**.

## Which chart is for what?

| What do you want to show?      | Chart                                         |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| change over time               | **line chart**                                |
| comparing categories           | **column** or **bar chart**                   |
| part of a whole (few parts)    | **pie** / doughnut chart (at most 5–6 slices) |
| relationship of two quantities | **scatter plot**                              |
| distribution                   | **histogram**                                 |
| geographic data                | **map** (coloured regions)                    |
| process, steps                 | **flowchart**, timeline                       |

## Infographics

An **infographic** combines data, text and images into an easy-to-scan picture that **tells a story** (e.g. “How does an e-mail reach its recipient?”, “60% of students study in the evening”).

A good infographic:

1. tells **one message**, and its title says it,
2. follows a **reading direction** (top to bottom or left to right),
3. uses few matching **colours** and at most two fonts,
4. highlights the point with **icons** and big numbers,
5. gives the **source** of its data.

## Cloud tools

| Tool                                         | What for?                                           |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Canva**                                    | infographics, posters, presentations from templates |
| **Piktochart**, **Venngage**                 | infographics                                        |
| **Google Sheets**, **Excel online**          | quick charts from a spreadsheet                     |
| **Power BI**, **Looker Studio**, **Tableau** | interactive business **dashboards** from live data  |
| **Miro**, **FigJam**                         | shared online whiteboard: mind maps, brainstorming  |

A **dashboard** shows several charts on one screen and refreshes on its own as the data source updates – that is how companies follow sales, traffic and errors.

## Misleading charts

- **Truncated axis**: if a column chart's y axis doesn't start at 0, a 2% difference looks huge.
- **Too many pie slices**: nobody can read anything from 12 slices.
- **3D effect**: distorts proportions.
- **Data without a source**: can't be checked.

## Summary

- Time → line; comparison → column; proportion → pie (few slices); relationship → scatter.
- An infographic tells one message with data, icons and a source.
- Tools: Canva, Sheets/Excel, Power BI, Looker Studio, Miro; beware of truncated axes.
