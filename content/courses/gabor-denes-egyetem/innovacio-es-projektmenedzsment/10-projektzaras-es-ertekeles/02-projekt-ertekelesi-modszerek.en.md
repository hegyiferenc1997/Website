## What you will learn

- when we can say a project was **successful**,
- what the **final report** contains,
- how to collect **lessons learned** (retrospectives),
- what **evaluating benefits afterwards** means.

## When is a project successful?

Success can be measured on several levels:

| Level                             | Question                                                             | When can it be measured? |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **project management success**    | Was it delivered on time, within budget, with the scope and quality? | at closure               |
| **product (deliverable) success** | Does the system work, is it used, are people satisfied?              | months later             |
| **business success**              | Were the benefits promised in the business case realised?            | possibly years later     |

> A project can be **a failure in management terms yet a business success** (e.g. it was late and more expensive but brought huge benefits) – and vice versa: a system finished on time that nobody uses.

## The final report

Typical contents of a project **final report**:

1. summary: goal, result, success in brief,
2. **scope** delivered (what was built, what was left out and why),
3. **schedule**: planned and actual finish, main reasons for variances,
4. **cost**: planned and actual cost, final CPI,
5. **quality**: acceptance test, open defects,
6. evaluation of **KPIs and success criteria**,
7. **risks** and issues: what happened, how we handled it,
8. **lessons learned** and recommendations,
9. open items and their owners.

### Evaluating KPIs

Every success criterion is compared with its target. Example:

| KPI               | Target      | Actual      | Met?              |
| ----------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ----------------- |
| deadline          | 31 Mar 2027 | 14 Apr 2027 | ❌ (2 weeks late) |
| cost (CPI)        | ≥ 0.95      | 1.02        | ✅                |
| page load time    | < 2 s       | 1.6 s       | ✅                |
| user satisfaction | ≥ 4.0       | 4.3         | ✅                |

## Lessons learned

Collecting **lessons learned** is the most valuable but most often neglected step of a project. Good practice:

- do **not only collect them at the end** – also per phase, per sprint,
- **blameless**: examine the process, do not look for people to blame,
- give concrete, **actionable** recommendations ("validate requirements with a prototype"), not generalities ("communicate better"),
- keep a **knowledge base** where the next project will find them.

### Retrospective formats

- **Start – Stop – Continue**: what to start, what to stop, what to continue.
- **What went well? What went wrong? What do we change?**
- **4L**: Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for.
- **Timeline**: draw the project's events together and mark the mood.

## Evaluating benefits afterwards

**Benefits realisation** examines whether the project's result – **after closure** – really brings the benefits promised in the business case. This is often done in a **Post-Implementation Review** (PIR), e.g. 3–12 months after roll-out.

Example: the business case promised savings of 12 million a year. One year after roll-out the measured saving is 9 million → **75% of the benefits** have been realised.

## Summary

- Three levels of success: project management, product and business success.
- Final report: scope, schedule, cost, quality, KPIs, risks, lessons learned.
- Lessons learned: continuously, blameless, actionable, in a knowledge base.
- Retrospectives: Start–Stop–Continue, 4L, timeline.
- Evaluating benefits afterwards (PIR): were the promised benefits realised?

## Lesson exercise

```quiz required
? A system was finished on time and within budget, but nobody uses it. At which level is the project a **failure**?
- at the project management level
+ at the product and business levels
- at no level, it is a success
- only at the cost level
! On time and on budget = management success; use and benefit are missing.

? What does **blameless** collection of lessons learned mean?
+ we look for faults in the process, not for a scapegoat
- nobody may say anything negative
- only the manager may speak
- mistakes are not documented
! That way people say honestly what happened.

? Which retrospective format asks: what should we start, stop and continue?
+ Start – Stop – Continue
- 4L
- SWOT
- MoSCoW
! Three simple questions.

? The business case promised savings of 20 million a year; the measured saving is 15 million. What percentage of the benefits has been realised?
= 75
= 75%
! 15 / 20 · 100.

? Which lesson learned is **actionable**?
- We need to communicate better.
- Let's be smarter.
+ In the next project, validate requirements with a clickable prototype before development.
- There should be no problems.
! A concrete, executable recommendation.
```
