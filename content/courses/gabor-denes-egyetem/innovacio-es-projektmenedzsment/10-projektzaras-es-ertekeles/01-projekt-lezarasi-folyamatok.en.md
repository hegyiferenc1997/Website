## What you will learn

- the steps of **closing** a project,
- how **handover and acceptance** and **acceptance testing** work,
- what **contract** and **administrative** closure are,
- when and how a project must be **closed early**.

## Why does closure matter?

Many projects do not "end", they just fade away: the team is already working on something else, the client has not signed the acceptance, invoices stay open, lessons are lost. Deliberate closure ensures that

- the client **formally accepts** the result,
- contracts and finances are **settled**,
- the organisation **learns** from the project (next lesson).

## The closure steps

```text
 1. acceptance test → 2. handover and acceptance → 3. transfer to operations
   → 4. contract closure → 5. administrative closure → 6. release the team
   → 7. evaluation, lessons learned → 8. celebration
```

### 1–2. Acceptance test and handover

- **User Acceptance Test** (UAT): the client checks on their real processes whether the system meets the **acceptance criteria** (topic 8).
- Defects found are sorted by **severity**: critical ones must be fixed before handover; minor ones may go on a **punch list** for later fixing.
- Acceptance is recorded in a **handover and acceptance record**, with signatures (sign-off). From then on the result **belongs to the client**.

### 3. Transfer to operations

After the project an IT system goes into **ongoing operations** (topic 6: project ≠ operations). We must hand over:

- the operations **documentation** (installation, backup, restore, monitoring),
- **access rights** and passwords, securely,
- **knowledge** (training, shadowing),
- the **warranty** and support agreement (SLA).

### 4. Contract closure

Closing supplier and subcontractor contracts: confirming delivery, paying final invoices, settling open disputes, recording warranties.

### 5. Administrative closure

- summarising final **costs** and **indicators** (CPI, SPI – topic 9),
- **archiving** project documentation (where it is, who can access it),
- closing project codes and cost centres, revoking access rights.

### 6. Releasing the team

Team members return to the line organisation or move to a new project. The PM gives feedback on their performance to their line managers and **thanks** them for their work. **Celebrating** is not a luxury: it builds motivation for the next project.

## Early termination

A project can be closed not only by reaching its goal. Reasons for early termination include:

- the **business case** has disappeared (e.g. strategy changed, a competitor already launched) – PRINCE2: continued business justification,
- the project has **hopelessly** exceeded its time or cost limits,
- the technology **does not work** (the Stage-Gate "kill" decision, topic 4),
- a change in the law or an external event.

> A stopped project must also be **closed properly**: document why it was stopped, salvage usable partial results and draw the lessons. **Sunk cost** – money already spent – is no argument for continuing.

## Summary

- Closure: acceptance test → handover (sign-off) → transfer to operations → contract closure → administrative closure → release team → lessons learned, celebration.
- With the acceptance record the result becomes the client's.
- Early termination: business case gone, hopeless overrun, technology not working; sunk cost is no argument.
