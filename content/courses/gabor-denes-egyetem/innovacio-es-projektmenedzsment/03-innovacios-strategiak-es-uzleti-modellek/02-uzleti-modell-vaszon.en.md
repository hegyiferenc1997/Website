## What you will learn

- what a **business model** means,
- the nine building blocks of the **Business Model Canvas**,
- how to fill it in for a computing product.

## What is a business model?

According to **Alexander Osterwalder**, a **business model** describes **how an organisation creates, delivers and captures value** (that is, how it makes money). A good product can fail with a bad business model – and an average product can succeed with a clever one.

## The Business Model Canvas

Osterwalder and Pigneur (2010) show the model on a single page with **nine building blocks**:

```text
┌────────────┬────────────┬────────────┬────────────┬────────────┐
│ Key        │ Key        │            │ Customer   │ Customer   │
│ partners   │ activities │   Value    │relationships│ segments  │
│   (8)      │    (7)     │ proposition│    (4)     │    (1)     │
│            ├────────────┤    (2)     ├────────────┤            │
│            │ Key        │            │ Channels   │            │
│            │ resources  │            │    (3)     │            │
│            │   (6)      │            │            │            │
├────────────┴────────────┴─────┬──────┴────────────┴────────────┤
│        Cost structure (9)     │         Revenue streams (5)    │
└───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

| #   | Block                      | Question                                                                     |
| --- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Customer segments**      | For whom do we create value? Who are our most important customers?           |
| 2   | **Value proposition**      | Which problem do we solve, which need do we satisfy?                         |
| 3   | **Channels**               | How do we reach customers (sales, delivery)?                                 |
| 4   | **Customer relationships** | What relationship do we build with them (self-service, personal, community)? |
| 5   | **Revenue streams**        | For what and how do customers pay?                                           |
| 6   | **Key resources**          | What do we need (people, technology, intellectual property, money)?          |
| 7   | **Key activities**         | What must we do best (development, operations, marketing)?                   |
| 8   | **Key partners**           | Who are our suppliers and allies?                                            |
| 9   | **Cost structure**         | What are our biggest costs?                                                  |

> The right side (1–5) is the side of **value and revenue** (towards the customer); the left side (6–9) is the side of **efficiency and cost** (operations). Always start filling it in with the **customer segments and the value proposition**.

## Example: a web platform supporting university studies

| Block                  | Content                                                   |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer segments      | computer engineering students; universities later         |
| Value proposition      | interactive lessons and exercises tailored to the courses |
| Channels               | website, social media, student organisations              |
| Customer relationships | self-service, automatic feedback, community forum         |
| Revenue streams        | per-semester subscription                                 |
| Key resources          | course content, platform code, authors                    |
| Key activities         | content creation, development, marketing                  |
| Key partners           | cloud provider, payment provider, lecturers               |
| Cost structure         | developer salaries, hosting, payment fees, marketing      |

## A related tool: the Value Proposition Canvas

The **Value Proposition Canvas** zooms into blocks 1 and 2: it sets the customer's **jobs, pains and gains** against the product's **pain relievers and gain creators**. When the two fit, you have **product–market fit**.

## Summary

- A business model describes how the organisation creates, delivers and captures value.
- The nine blocks of the Business Model Canvas: customer segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners, cost structure.
- Filling it in starts with the customers and the value proposition.
