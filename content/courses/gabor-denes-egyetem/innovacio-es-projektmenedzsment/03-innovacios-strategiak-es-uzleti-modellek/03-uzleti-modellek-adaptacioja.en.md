## What you will learn

- the most common **business model patterns** in computing,
- what **adapting** a business model (adopting and reshaping it) means,
- how to test your idea cheaply with the **Lean Startup** method.

## Common business model patterns

| Pattern                         | Essence                                                           | Example                                 |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **subscription**                | a recurring fee for continuous access                             | Netflix, Spotify, Microsoft 365         |
| **freemium**                    | the basic service is free, extra features are paid                | Dropbox, Duolingo, Canva                |
| **platform (two-sided market)** | connects two (or more) user groups and lives on a commission      | Uber, Airbnb, App Store                 |
| **razor and blades**            | the base device is cheap, consumables or content bring the profit | printer and ink, game console and games |
| **long tail**                   | many products with small sales each add up to large revenue       | Amazon, streaming catalogues            |
| **advertising-based**           | free for the user, advertisers pay                                | Google Search, YouTube                  |
| **open source + services**      | the software is free; support, hosting or cloud is paid           | Red Hat, GitLab                         |

> The key metric of freemium is the **conversion rate**: what percentage of users become paying users. If 300 out of 10,000 free users pay, conversion is **3%**.

## Adapting a business model

**Adaptation** = **adopting a model proven elsewhere and tailoring it** to your own situation. According to the **St. Gallen Business Model Navigator** (Gassmann et al.), about 90% of business model innovations are new combinations of existing patterns. Its steps:

1. **Initiation** – draw the current model (Business Model Canvas).
2. **Ideation** – which patterns could apply? ("What if it were a subscription?")
3. **Integration** – fit the new model with the company's resources and partners.
4. **Implementation** – experiment, roll out gradually.

Example: much of the software market switched from **boxed licences** to **SaaS subscriptions** – more predictable revenue for the company, a lower entry cost for the customer.

## The Lean Startup method

**Eric Ries**' (2011) method for uncertain new ventures: do not plan for years, **test your assumptions quickly and cheaply**.

```text
     ┌──────► BUILD (product) ─────┐
     │                             ▼
  LEARN (ideas) ◄────── MEASURE (data)
```

- **MVP** (Minimum Viable Product): the simplest version with which an assumption can already be tested (it may even be a demo video or a manually run "automated" service).
- **Build – measure – learn** loop: the faster it cycles, the faster the team learns.
- **Validated learning**: decisions are based on real user data, not opinions.
- **Pivot**: if the data shows an assumption is wrong, change one element of the strategy (e.g. a different customer segment or revenue model) while keeping what was learnt. Its opposite is to **persevere**.
- **A/B test**: show two versions to two user groups and measure which performs better.

## Summary

- Common patterns: subscription, freemium, platform, razor and blades, long tail, advertising, open source + services.
- Adaptation: adopting and tailoring a proven pattern; most business model innovations combine existing patterns.
- Lean Startup: MVP, build–measure–learn, validated learning, pivot, A/B test.
