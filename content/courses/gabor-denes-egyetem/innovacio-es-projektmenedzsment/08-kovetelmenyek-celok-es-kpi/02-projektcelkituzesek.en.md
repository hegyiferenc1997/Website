## What you will learn

- how the project objective connects to the **organisation's strategy**,
- how to phrase a **SMART** objective,
- what **OKR** is and how it differs from SMART.

## The hierarchy of goals

```text
 strategic goal      → "Become the best digital university in the region."
   └─ project objective → "From the spring 2027 semester, the new course
                           registration system halves registration complaints."
        └─ deliverables → web app, training material, data migration
             └─ activities → design, development, testing …
```

- The **project objective** says **what change** we want to achieve.
- **Deliverables** are the "tangible" things the project creates.

> A common mistake is writing a deliverable instead of the objective ("an app is built"). An app can be built that nobody uses – the objective is the **impact**.

## SMART objectives

The five criteria of **SMART** (George T. Doran, 1981):

| Letter | Meaning    | Question                                       |
| ------ | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **S**  | Specific   | What exactly do we want to achieve?            |
| **M**  | Measurable | How do we know we succeeded? With what number? |
| **A**  | Achievable | Is it realistic with the available resources?  |
| **R**  | Relevant   | Does it fit the strategy? Does it matter?      |
| **T**  | Time-bound | By when?                                       |

### Bad and good objective

- ❌ "Improve the website." – not specific, not measurable, no deadline.
- ✅ "By 31 March 2027, reduce the web shop's mobile page load time from 4 s to under 2 s, with the current development team."

It is specific (mobile load time), measurable (2 s), achievable (the team exists), relevant (customers are lost because of the slow site) and time-bound (31 March 2027).

## OKR – Objectives and Key Results

The **OKR** (Objectives and Key Results) method was developed at Intel (Andy Grove) and made popular by Google.

- **Objective**: an **inspiring**, qualitative direction – not a number.
- **Key Results**: 2–5 **measurable** results showing whether we are getting closer to the objective.

Example:

> **Objective**: Students love using the registration system.
>
> - **KR1**: satisfaction score rises from 3.2 to 4.5 (on a 5-point scale),
> - **KR2**: the number of registration-related support tickets falls by 50%,
> - **KR3**: 90% of registrations complete without errors on mobile.

OKRs usually run in **quarterly** cycles and are deliberately **ambitious**: 70% achievement already counts as a good result. A SMART objective, by contrast, is something that **must be met**.

## Summary

- Strategic goal → project objective (impact) → deliverables → activities.
- SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound.
- OKR: inspiring objective + 2–5 measurable key results, quarterly, ambitious (70% is already good).
