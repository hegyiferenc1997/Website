## What you will learn

- how innovations are grouped by their **subject** (what we renew),
- how they are grouped by their **degree** (how big the change is),
- what **disruptive** innovation is, and the difference between **closed** and **open** innovation.

## What do we renew? – types by subject

The earlier (2005) edition of the Oslo Manual distinguished four types; this classification is still widely used:

| Type                          | What changes?                                          | Computing example                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **product innovation**        | the product or service itself                          | a new mobile app, a faster processor              |
| **process innovation**        | the way it is produced or delivered                    | automated testing and deployment (CI/CD)          |
| **marketing innovation**      | the product's design, price, sales or promotion        | subscription pricing instead of boxed software    |
| **organisational innovation** | work organisation, decision making, external relations | switching to agile teams, introducing remote work |

> The 2018 edition simplified this into two big groups: **product innovation** and **business process innovation** (the latter includes marketing and organisational innovation).

## How big is the change? – types by degree

- **Incremental** innovation: improving an existing product or process **in small steps**. Low risk, predictable benefit. Example: the phone gets a better camera every year.
- **Radical** (breakthrough) innovation: a **fundamentally new** solution that can create a new market or industry. High risk, high potential benefit. Example: the first web search engine, generative AI.
- **Architectural** innovation: a **new combination** of existing components (e.g. the smartphone = phone + camera + GPS + computer).

## Disruptive innovation

Described by **Clayton Christensen** (Harvard): a **disruptive** innovation is at first **simpler, cheaper** and weaker than the market leader's product, so big companies do not take it seriously. It starts in a neglected or new customer segment, **gradually catches up** and finally pushes out the incumbents.

Examples:

- the personal computer versus mainframes,
- digital photography versus film (the Kodak case),
- cloud services versus the company's own server room.

> Disruptive is not the same as radical: disruption is about **market impact** (attacking the leader from below), radicalness is about the degree of **technological novelty**.

## Closed and open innovation

- **Closed innovation**: the company develops everything with its **own** R&D department, in secret.
- **Open innovation** (**Henry Chesbrough**, 2003): the company deliberately uses **external** ideas and knowledge (universities, start-ups, users) and also lets its unused ideas out (licensing, spin-offs). Examples: open-source software, hackathons, idea competitions, crowdsourcing.

## Summary

- By subject: product, process, marketing and organisational innovation.
- By degree: incremental (small step), radical (breakthrough), architectural (new combination).
- Disruptive innovation pushes out the leader from below with a cheaper, simpler solution.
- Open innovation: deliberately involving external ideas and partners.
