## What you will learn

- what **creativity** is and whether it can be developed,
- what **divergent** and **convergent** thinking are,
- how to use the best-known **creativity techniques** in a team.

## What is creativity?

**Creativity** is the ability to produce ideas that are **new and useful**. It is the first step of innovation: without creativity there is no idea to implement.

Creativity is **not an innate gift** but something that can be learnt and developed. It is supported by:

- **expertise** (the more you know about a field, the more elements you can combine in new ways),
- **intrinsic motivation** (you care about the problem),
- a **supportive environment** (mistakes are allowed, no instant criticism).

## Divergent and convergent thinking

Every creative process alternates between two phases:

| Phase          | Goal                                              | Rule                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **divergent**  | as **many** ideas as possible, in many directions | do not judge, quantity matters       |
| **convergent** | selecting the **best** ideas                      | evaluate, filter, decide on criteria |

> The most common mistake is mixing the two: if ideas are criticised immediately, people fall silent.

## Brainstorming

The method of **Alex Osborn** (1950s). A group of 5–10 people collects ideas on a well-phrased question. Its four rules:

1. **No criticism** during ideation.
2. **Quantity counts** – the more ideas, the better the chance of a good one.
3. **Wild ideas are welcome** – breakthroughs come from the unusual.
4. **Build on others' ideas** – combine and improve them.

A variant is **brainwriting** (e.g. the 6-3-5 method): 6 people write 3 ideas on a sheet every 5 minutes, then pass it on – so quieter members are heard too. After 6 rounds, 6 · 3 · 6 = **108** ideas are produced.

## SCAMPER

A checklist of questions for rethinking an existing product or process (Bob Eberle):

| Letter | Question                                          | Example (a note-taking app)       |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **S**  | _Substitute_ – what could I replace?              | voice recording instead of typing |
| **C**  | _Combine_ – what could I combine?                 | notes + calendar                  |
| **A**  | _Adapt_ – what could I borrow from elsewhere?     | the levelling system of games     |
| **M**  | _Modify_ – what could I enlarge or change?        | shared, real-time editing         |
| **P**  | _Put to other use_ – what else is it good for?    | exam-prep flashcards              |
| **E**  | _Eliminate_ – what could I leave out?             | use without registration          |
| **R**  | _Reverse / Rearrange_ – what could I turn around? | quiz first, lesson after          |

## Six Thinking Hats

**Edward de Bono**'s method: the whole group wears **the same "hat"** at a time, so viewpoints do not get mixed.

| Hat        | Viewpoint                             |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **white**  | facts, data, information              |
| **red**    | feelings, intuition                   |
| **black**  | risks, criticism, what could go wrong |
| **yellow** | benefits, optimism                    |
| **green**  | new ideas, creativity                 |
| **blue**   | managing the process, summarising     |

De Bono also coined **lateral thinking**: approaching a problem "from the side" instead of along the usual logical path.

## Mind maps and design thinking

- **Mind map** (Tony Buzan): branches start from a central concept, so associations can be organised visually.
- **Design thinking** (IDEO, Stanford d.school): a human- (user-) centred innovation process in five steps: **empathise → define the problem → ideate → prototype → test**. The steps repeat (iteration), and prototypes are quick and cheap.

## Summary

- Creativity can be developed; the divergent (many ideas) and convergent (selection) phases must be kept apart.
- Brainstorming: no criticism, quantity, wild ideas, building on others.
- SCAMPER: seven questions for rethinking an existing solution.
- Six hats: white facts, red feelings, black risks, yellow benefits, green ideas, blue control.
- Design thinking: empathise, define, ideate, prototype, test.
