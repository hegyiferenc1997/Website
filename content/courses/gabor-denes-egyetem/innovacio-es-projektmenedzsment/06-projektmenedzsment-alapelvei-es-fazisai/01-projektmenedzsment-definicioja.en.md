## What you will learn

- what a **project** is, and what is **not** a project,
- what **project management** means,
- what the **project triangle** (iron triangle) is,
- the best-known **standards** and their principles (PMBOK, PRINCE2, IPMA).

## What is a project?

According to the PMBOK Guide of the **PMI** (Project Management Institute):

> A project is a **temporary** endeavour undertaken to create a **unique** product, service or result.

Characteristics of a project:

- **temporary** – it has a start and an end (the end may be reaching the goal or stopping the project),
- **unique** – its result is new in some way, not made like this before,
- **goal-oriented** – it exists to achieve a defined goal,
- **resource-constrained** – a given amount of time, money and people is available,
- **risky** – novelty brings uncertainty.

### Project or ongoing operations?

| Project                        | Ongoing operations                                     |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| temporary, has an end          | permanent, repetitive                                  |
| unique result                  | the same result again and again                        |
| e.g. building a new web shop   | e.g. running the web shop day to day, customer service |
| e.g. introducing an ERP system | e.g. monthly payroll                                   |

## What is project management?

**Project management** is the application of knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities to meet the project requirements. In short: **planning, organising, leading and controlling** the project to achieve its goal.

The **project manager** is responsible for achieving the project goal; they lead the team and report to the sponsor (roles: topic 12).

## The project triangle

According to the classic **iron triangle** (triple constraint), the three main constraints of a project are linked:

```text
                 scope
                  /\
                 /  \
                /qual-\
               / ity   \
              /__________\
          time             cost
```

- **Scope** – what must be delivered.
- **Time** – the deadline, the schedule.
- **Cost** – the budget.

**Quality** sits in the middle. If one side changes, at least one other must change too: e.g. if more features are needed (scope ↑), that needs either more time or more money – otherwise quality suffers.

> The **agile** approach (topic 11) inverts the triangle: it **fixes time and cost** (fixed sprint length, fixed team) and lets **scope** vary.

## Standards and principles

| Standard        | Publisher                                    | Essence                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PMBOK Guide** | PMI (USA)                                    | the 7th edition (2021) describes **12 principles** and **8 performance domains**; earlier editions were built on 5 process groups and 10 knowledge areas |
| **PRINCE2**     | AXELOS (UK)                                  | a process-based method with **7 principles**, 7 themes and 7 processes                                                                                   |
| **IPMA ICB**    | International Project Management Association | describes the project manager's **competences**                                                                                                          |
| **ISO 21502**   | ISO                                          | international guidance on project management                                                                                                             |

**The seven PRINCE2 principles**: continued business justification; learn from experience; defined roles and responsibilities; manage by stages; manage by exception; focus on products; tailor to suit the project environment.

**Some PMBOK 7 principles**: be a diligent, respectful and caring steward; create a collaborative team environment; engage stakeholders; focus on value; tailor the approach to the context; optimise risk responses; embrace adaptability and resiliency; enable change.

## Summary

- Project = a temporary endeavour to create a unique result; operations are repetitive.
- Project management = planning, organising, leading and controlling the project.
- Iron triangle: scope, time, cost (quality in the middle) – they are linked.
- Standards: PMBOK (PMI), PRINCE2 (7 principles), IPMA ICB (competences), ISO 21502.
