## What you will learn

- which **phases** a project goes through,
- what happens in the **five process groups**,
- how **effort**, **risk** and the **cost of change** evolve during a project.

## The project life cycle

The **project life cycle** is the series of phases a project passes through from start to closure. The five PMBOK **process groups**:

```text
 1. Initiating → 2. Planning → 3. Executing → 5. Closing
                      ▲             │
                      └─────────────┘
            4. Monitoring & Controlling (throughout)
```

### 1. Initiating

- stating the need and the business goal (**business case**),
- feasibility study,
- drafting and approving the **project charter** – this officially starts the project and authorises the project manager,
- identifying **stakeholders** (topic 13).

### 2. Planning

- refining the scope, the **work breakdown structure** (WBS),
- the schedule (activities, dependencies, Gantt chart, critical path),
- budget, resource plan, risk management plan, communication plan, quality plan.

Planning is covered in detail in topics 7–8.

### 3. Executing

- the team does the work and the **deliverables** are produced,
- the project manager coordinates, communicates, manages the team and suppliers.

This is the phase with the highest **effort** (cost, headcount).

### 4. Monitoring & Controlling

Runs **in parallel** with the others throughout:

- comparing plan and actual (schedule, cost, quality),
- **corrective action** when there is a deviation,
- handling **change requests** (topic 14).

### 5. Closing

- **handover and acceptance** of the result,
- closing contracts, releasing the team,
- collecting **lessons learned**, archiving (topic 10).

## Effort, risk and influence over the life cycle

```text
 high │ ●●                       effort: ▁▃▆█▆▂
      │   ●●      ▄▄▄             (highest during execution)
      │     ●● ▄▀    ▀▄
      │      ▄●●       ▀▄         ● ability to influence and risk:
      │    ▄▀   ●●       ▀▄         highest at the start,
 low  │ ▄▀▀       ●●●●●●●●●▀▀▄     falling towards the end
      └──────────────────────────▶ time
        init. plan. executing  closing
```

- At the **start** of the project **uncertainty and risk** are highest, but this is where the outcome is easiest to **influence**.
- The **cost of change** grows as the project progresses: what is a rewritten sentence in the specification during planning may be weeks of rework at the end of execution.

> That is why it pays to spend time on initiating and planning: **early mistakes are the cheapest**.

## Phases and gates

Larger projects are split into several **phases** (e.g. analysis, design, development, testing, roll-out). At the end of every phase a **phase-end review** (phase gate) decides on continuing – the same idea as the Stage-Gate model (topic 4) and PRINCE2's "manage by stages" principle.

## Summary

- Five process groups: initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, closing.
- The main output of initiating is the project charter; of planning, the project plan.
- Effort is highest during execution; risk and influence are highest at the start.
- The cost of change grows as the project progresses.
