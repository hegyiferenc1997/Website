## What you will learn

- which problem **agile** thinking answered,
- the four values of the **Agile Manifesto**,
- the most important **agile principles**,
- what **iterative** and **incremental** development mean.

## Why was it born?

In the 1990s many software projects were run with the traditional method of planning everything up front. A common experience was that

- by the time the system was finished (after years), **requirements had changed**,
- the client only saw the result at the end – and realised it was **not what they wanted**,
- detailed documentation took a lot of time but **went out of date quickly**.

In response, several "lightweight" methods appeared (Scrum, Extreme Programming, Crystal …), and in **February 2001** 17 software developers at the Snowbird ski resort in Utah wrote the **Manifesto for Agile Software Development**.

## The four values

> We value:
>
> - **individuals and interactions** over processes and tools,
> - **working software** over comprehensive documentation,
> - **customer collaboration** over contract negotiation,
> - **responding to change** over following a plan.
>
> That is, while there is value in the items on the right, **we value the items on the left more**.

Important: the manifesto does **not** say there is no need for documentation or plans – only that the items on the left matter more.

## Some of the twelve principles

1. Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through **early and continuous** delivery of valuable software.
2. Welcome **changing requirements**, even late in development.
3. Deliver **working software frequently**, every few weeks or months.
4. Business people and developers must **work together daily**.
5. Build projects around **motivated individuals**; give them the environment and trust them.
6. The most efficient communication is **face-to-face conversation**.
7. **Working software** is the primary measure of progress.
8. **Sustainable pace**: the team should be able to work indefinitely, without burning out.
9. **Simplicity** – maximising the amount of work not done – is essential.
10. The best solutions emerge from **self-organising teams**.
11. At **regular intervals** the team reflects on how to become more effective and **adjusts** (retrospective).

## Iterative and incremental development

- **Incremental**: the product is built **piece by piece**; every step adds another usable piece (increment).
- **Iterative**: we work in short, repeated cycles (**iterations**, in Scrum **sprints**), and after every cycle we **refine** based on feedback.

```text
 waterfall: [ analysis ][ design ][ development ][ test ] → value only at the end
 agile:     [sprint 1]→[sprint 2]→[sprint 3]→[sprint 4]   → value after every sprint
              ▲ feedback   ▲ feedback   ▲ feedback
```

Agile thus **reduces risk early**: if an idea is wrong, it shows after 2–4 weeks, not after two years.

## Summary

- Agile thinking answers changing requirements and late feedback.
- Agile Manifesto (2001): individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, responding to change.
- 12 principles: early and continuous delivery, welcoming change, daily collaboration, self-organisation, sustainable pace, regular reflection.
- Iterative (repeated cycles) and incremental (piece-by-piece) development.
