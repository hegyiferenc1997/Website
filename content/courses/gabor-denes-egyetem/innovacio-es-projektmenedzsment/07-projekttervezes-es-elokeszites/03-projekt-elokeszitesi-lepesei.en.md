## What you will learn

- which steps precede the **official start** of a project,
- what the **business case** and the **feasibility study** contain,
- what goes into the **project charter**,
- how the **kick-off meeting** works.

## The preparation steps

```text
 1. need/idea → 2. business case → 3. feasibility → 4. decision
   → 5. charter → 6. appoint PM, team → 7. kick-off meeting
```

## 1–2. Need and business case

Every project starts from a **need**: a problem, an opportunity or a legal obligation. The **business case** justifies that the project is **worth** starting:

- description of the problem or opportunity,
- possible **solution options** (including the "do nothing" option),
- expected **benefits**, **costs** and **risks**,
- financial indicators (payback, ROI, NPV – topic 4),
- recommendation.

## 3. Feasibility study – TELOS

The **feasibility study** examines whether the project **can** be done. The five areas of **TELOS**:

| Letter | Area        | Question                                                       |
| ------ | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **T**  | Technical   | Do we have the technology and the expertise?                   |
| **E**  | Economic    | Is it worth it? Is there money for it?                         |
| **L**  | Legal       | Does it comply with the law (GDPR, licences)?                  |
| **O**  | Operational | Does it fit the organisation's operations? Will people use it? |
| **S**  | Scheduling  | Can it be done by the required deadline?                       |

## 4–5. Decision and the project charter

If the decision makers (sponsor, management) approve, the **project charter** is drawn up. It is a short (1–3 page) document that **officially starts** the project and **authorises** the project manager. Typical contents:

- the project's **name**, **goal** and **justification**,
- main **deliverables** and scope boundaries (what is **not** included),
- main **milestones** and the deadline,
- the summary **budget**,
- main **risks** and **assumptions**,
- the main **stakeholders**,
- the names of the **sponsor** and the **project manager**, the PM's authority,
- **success measures** (acceptance criteria, KPIs – topic 8),
- approval signatures.

> **Assumption**: something we take as true when planning, but is not certain (e.g. "the client replies within 5 working days"). **Constraint**: a fixed condition (e.g. "the budget is 20 million").

## 6. The project manager and the team

The sponsor appoints the **project manager**, who assembles the **core team**. This is also when the form of the **project organisation** is decided (topic 12).

## 7. The kick-off meeting

The **kick-off meeting** is the "starting gun" where the team and the main stakeholders meet for the first time. Its agenda:

1. introductions, roles and responsibilities,
2. the project's goal, scope and background,
3. main milestones, schedule,
4. communication rules (meetings, tools, reports),
5. risks and open questions,
6. next steps.

Its purpose is to create **shared understanding** and **commitment**.

## Summary

- Preparation: need → business case → feasibility → decision → charter → team → kick-off.
- Business case: is it worth it? Feasibility (TELOS): can it be done?
- The charter officially starts the project and authorises the PM.
- The kick-off creates shared understanding and commitment.
