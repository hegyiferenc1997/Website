## What you will learn

- which **stages** a team goes through (Tuckman),
- which **roles** a good team needs (Belbin),
- how to **handle conflict** (Thomas–Kilmann),
- what **motivates** people and what **psychological safety** is.

## Group and team

A **group** is a collection of people; it becomes a **team** when they have a **common goal**, **depend on each other** and are **jointly responsible** for the result. A project's success often comes not from the best individuals but from the team that works together best.

## Stages of team development (Tuckman, 1965)

| Stage                 | Characteristic                                            | The PM's task                                        |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **forming**           | politeness, uncertainty, everyone looking for their place | clarify goals, roles, rules (directing)              |
| **storming**          | arguments, conflicts about tasks and power                | conflict management, listening (coaching)            |
| **norming**           | shared rules emerge, trust grows                          | support, hand over responsibility                    |
| **performing**        | efficient, autonomous, coordinated work                   | delegate, remove obstacles                           |
| **adjourning** (1977) | end of the project, farewell                              | recognition, lessons learned, celebration (topic 10) |

> **Storming** is natural and necessary: a team that skips it (suppresses arguments) rarely reaches real performance.

## Belbin's team roles

According to **Meredith Belbin**'s research, successful teams have nine roles present (one person may fill several):

| Group        | Roles                                                                                                                    |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **thinking** | **Plant** – creative; **Monitor Evaluator** – critical analyst; **Specialist** – deep expertise                          |
| **action**   | **Shaper** – driving force; **Implementer** – turns plans into action; **Completer Finisher** – precise, deadline-driven |
| **people**   | **Co-ordinator** – leads, delegates; **Teamworker** – creates harmony; **Resource Investigator** – builds contacts       |

If a role is missing (e.g. no "completer finisher"), the team makes characteristic mistakes (e.g. work gets stuck at 90%).

## Conflict management – Thomas–Kilmann

Conflict in projects is **inevitable** (resources, priorities, deadlines, personalities). The **Thomas–Kilmann model** distinguishes five styles along two dimensions (how much I assert my own interest / how much I consider the other's):

| Style             | Essence                | When is it good?                                 |
| ----------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **competing**     | I win, you lose        | emergencies, a quick decision is needed          |
| **accommodating** | you win, I give in     | it matters less to me, we build the relationship |
| **avoiding**      | we do not deal with it | a trivial issue, or things need to cool down     |
| **compromising**  | both give up something | time pressure, equal parties                     |
| **collaborating** | both win (win–win)     | an important issue, time for a joint solution    |

In project management the **collaborating** (problem-solving) style usually gives the most lasting result.

## Motivation

- **Maslow**'s hierarchy of needs: physiological → safety → belonging → esteem → self-actualisation.
- **Herzberg**'s two-factor theory: lack of **hygiene factors** (pay, working conditions) causes dissatisfaction, but their presence does not motivate; **motivators** (recognition, responsibility, growth, the work itself) create commitment.
- **Daniel Pink**'s three elements of intrinsic motivation: **autonomy**, **mastery**, **purpose** – especially true for creative IT work.

## Psychological safety

According to Google's **Project Aristotle** (2015), the most important trait of successful teams is **psychological safety** (Amy Edmondson's concept): members **dare to ask questions, admit mistakes and raise new ideas** without fear of embarrassment or punishment. Blameless retrospectives (topic 10) and the leader's own example strengthen it.

## Remote and hybrid teams

- a shared, written **working agreement** (availability, response times, tools),
- **asynchronous** communication by default (documented decisions), synchronous meetings only when needed,
- deliberate **relationship building** (online coffee breaks, meeting in person every quarter),
- attention to **time zones** and cultural differences.

## Summary

- Tuckman: forming → storming → norming → performing → adjourning.
- Belbin: nine team roles in three groups (thinking, action, people).
- Thomas–Kilmann: competing, accommodating, avoiding, compromising, collaborating.
- Motivation: Maslow, Herzberg (hygiene factors and motivators), Pink (autonomy, mastery, purpose).
- Psychological safety: daring to ask and to make mistakes.
